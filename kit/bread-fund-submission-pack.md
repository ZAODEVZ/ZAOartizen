# Bread Fund submission pack - copy-paste material

Everything needed to get The ZAO into the **Bread Fund for the Solidarity Economy** on Artizen Season 7.
Research and the eligibility screen: `research/854-bread-fund-solidarity-economy/`.

Fund URL: `https://artizen.fund/index/mf/bread-fund-for-the-solidarity-economy?season=7`

## Before you send anything

Three checks, in order. Skipping these is how a qualifying submission gets rejected.

1. **The deadline in the original announcement is dead.** It closed 2026-08-06 18:00 UTC. Do not forward
   that message to the crew. Use the rally copy below instead, which points at the fund rather than at an
   expired drive.
2. **Confirm the two open VC questions** before submitting POIDH or Baraza TV. The fund's stated gate is
   "no VC funding" and that is a factual claim about someone else's cap table. Ask the owner. Do not
   assert it for them.
3. **Do not submit solo artists.** Marie Chain, InSync, Heritage Collection fail the 3+ member gate. They
   belong in Global Music and the ZAO Fund. Sending them here signals we did not read the criteria.

## The framing rule (read this before writing any submission)

This fund is run by people whose stated position is that venture capital ruined crypto. The selection
criterion is our STRUCTURE, not our genre.

- Lead with: member-owned, no VC, artists keep ownership, decisions made by members, built to outlast any
  one person.
- Do not lead with: platform, product, growth, users, web3 music tech.

Same facts, different order. The order is the whole submission.

## Step 1 - back the fund first (free, 5 minutes)

Do this before submitting. On a fund whose entire thesis is solidarity, arriving as a supporter before
arriving as an applicant is the correct read of the room.

Boosting costs nothing. Sponsor dollars unlock match from the Artizen Endowment for every project in the
fund, ours included once we are in.

## Step 2 - the submissions

Four projects pass the screen. Each blurb below is written structure-first per the framing rule.

### The ZAO / ZAOstock

> The ZAO is a member-owned music organization - 188 members, on Base, no venture capital, no label
> behind it. Members decide what gets funded and artists keep ownership of what they make. ZAOstock is
> the festival that comes out of that: a recurring, community-run event where the people who show up are
> the same people who govern the thing.
>
> We are not a platform that hosts a community. We are a community that built its own tools because the
> alternative was renting someone else's and giving up the masters.
>
> The ZAO also runs its own match fund on Artizen (the ZAO Fund for Emerging Culture), so we are on both
> sides of this: we fund emerging artists, and we are ourselves the kind of organization this fund exists
> to back.

### HuRya Empowerment Foundation (PolyRaiders / Moses)

> HuRya is a non-profit foundation using web3 art to fund dignity for girls in Nigeria - menstrual
> products, education, and now a vocational centre. 767 mints on Base reached 1,000 girls. Not a pilot,
> not a pitch deck: a delivered number.
>
> It is run as a foundation, not a startup. No VC, no exit. The funding model is the mutual aid model -
> people who have a little back people who need it, directly, and the art is what carries the money
> across.

### COC Concertz (Thy Revolution)

> COC Concertz is an artist-led live concert series run as a 50/50 joint venture between The ZAO and the
> COC, led by Thy Revolution. Five shows so far, streamed multi-platform.
>
> The structure is the point. Most concert series are owned by a promoter and the artists are booked
> labour. This one is co-owned - the artists running it hold half of it, and each concert becomes a
> collectible that funds the next one rather than a ticket stub that disappears. No VC, no promoter
> extracting the upside.

### Baraza TV (Motomoto / Aziz)

> Baraza TV is an Africa-focused media network out of Nairobi that pays African creators for African
> stories, rather than licensing them cheaply for someone else's platform.
>
> It is built as a creator commons: the people making the work are the people the money reaches. No
> venture funding, no extractive licensing layer. It exists because the default distribution deal for
> African creators is a bad one, and the fix was to build the alternative rather than negotiate with it.

### Artifact spec (all submissions)

Square 1:1, minimum 1000x1000px, video or GIF performs best, **no text or graphic overlay**. $10 open
edition, 0% fees, 100% to the creator, ETH wallet for payout.

## Step 3 - the director-to-director message

The highest-leverage item in this pack, and the only one that compounds. We run a fund; they run a fund.
Cross-curation between two funds is a symmetric trade, not a favour. Send this to the Bread Fund's
director or via the Artizen fund contact.

> Hi - I run the ZAO Fund for Emerging Culture on Artizen. Just found the Bread Fund and it is the first
> fund on the platform where the criteria describe our organization rather than our genre.
>
> The ZAO is a 188-member music DAO on Base. Member-owned, no VC, artists keep their masters. We have
> submitted a few projects that clear your gates - our own festival, a Nigerian foundation funding girls'
> education through art, an artist co-owned concert series, and a Nairobi creator network.
>
> The reason I am writing rather than just submitting: cross-curation is free match for both of us. Any
> project in either fund earns match from every fund backing it, so curating qualifying projects both
> directions costs nothing and multiplies what our two crowds unlock. Happy to start by curating Bread
> projects into the ZAO Fund whether or not ours come the other way.
>
> Either way, we boosted the fund. Good to see a solidarity-economy fund on there.

Note the last two lines. The offer is one-directional first, and the boost is already done before the ask
is made. That is the whole difference between a trade and a request.
