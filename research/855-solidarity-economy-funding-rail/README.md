# 855 - The solidarity economy funding rail: the Artizen Bread Fund is a door, not the room

Researched 2026-08-16, continuing from `research/854-bread-fund-solidarity-economy/`. Where 854 answered
"should we apply to this fund", this asks the larger question it opened: what else does qualifying as a
cooperative unlock, and what is behind the door.

## Executive summary

Applying to the Bread Fund on Artizen is a one-off shot at a match pool. Behind it sits something better:
**Bread Cooperative runs a perpetual, member-voted funding stream that pays out every 30 days**, and its
network of member projects has no music or culture organization in it.

That reframes the play. The Artizen fund is the introduction. **Membership in the Bread Cooperative
network is the actual prize**, because it converts a single drive into a recurring distribution we are
eligible for every month, indefinitely.

And there is a second, wider finding that has nothing to do with crypto: **The ZAO's real structural
peers are not other web3 music apps. They are music platform co-ops** - Ampled, Resonate, Groupmuse,
Subvert. That peer set comes with its own funders, its own institutions, and a 350+ entry funding library
we have never touched.

## The recurring rail: how the Bread Solidarity Fund actually pays

This is the mechanism worth understanding, because it is unlike Artizen's.

- Users "bake" BREAD by depositing xDAI, which is converted into **sDAI**, a yield-bearing asset.
- The depositor keeps their principal and can still spend the BREAD. **Only the yield is collectivized.**
- Every **30 days**, BREAD holders vote on how that cycle's yield is distributed across the network's
  member projects.
- Voting power in a cycle is based on a holder's **average holdings over the previous cycle**, which
  blocks last-minute vote-buying.
- Reported distribution: **over $40,000 since launch**, with over $20k of that across four recent voting
  cycles under a newer mechanism.

Two things follow. First, the amounts are modest - this is not a large grant program, and nobody should
plan a budget around it. Second, and more importantly, **it is recurring and it is voted by a community
rather than granted by a committee**. A member project with a crowd that shows up gets paid month after
month. That is a very different asset from winning one Artizen drive, and it happens to be exactly the
muscle The ZAO already has: mobilizing members to take a small free action on a schedule.

We have been building that muscle for the Artizen boost cycle all year. It transfers directly.

## The white space: there is no culture project in the network

Member projects, as best I can establish from search (see verification limits):

| Member project | What it is |
|---|---|
| Crypto Commons Association | commons research and gatherings |
| ReFi DAO / Regen Coordination | regenerative finance coordination |
| Citizen Wallet | local and community currency infrastructure |
| LaborDAO | labour organizing tooling |
| Symbiota Coop | cooperative tooling |
| Gardens | funding and governance infrastructure |
| Bread core team + shared treasury | the co-op itself |

Read that list as a portfolio and the gap is obvious: it is **all infrastructure and coordination, and no
culture**. No music, no festival, no artist organization.

That is the strongest argument we have, and it is not "please fund us". It is: *a federation arguing that
the solidarity economy is a real alternative needs a culture arm, and does not have one. We are a
188-member artist-owned music organization that has been operating as one for years. We would be your
first.*

A federation's breadth is its legitimacy. Being the first of a category is worth more to them than being
the seventh piece of infrastructure, and it is worth more to us than the money.

## How membership actually works

Two paths surfaced, and the distinction matters:

1. **Invited membership.** Projects demonstrate alignment with the co-op's stated principles - **dual
   power, economic democracy, mutual aid, and transparent governance** - and each proposal must show **how
   it benefits the broader Bread Cooperative ecosystem**, not just the applicant. Historically,
   applications went to a co-op contact address.
2. **Self-inclusion by staking.** External projects that believe they are aligned can include themselves
   by **staking 10,000 BREAD**. Members do not need to go through this.

The second path is interesting and should be treated carefully. It means membership is not purely
gatekept, but it also means buying in is a capital commitment in someone else's token. Per our standing
caution on ART, **do not put ZAO treasury into a governance token to buy a seat**. Earn the invitation
first; revisit staking only if the relationship is already real and the amount is trivial.

**The framing test.** "How does this benefit the broader ecosystem" is the criterion most applicants fail
by talking about themselves. Our answer is genuinely good: we bring a **crowd**, and a crowd is what a
monthly-vote funding mechanism runs on. 188 members who already show up on a schedule are a direct
contribution to the health of every other member project's funding cycle. We are not asking to draw from
the pool; we are offering to enlarge the participation that fills it.

## The peer-set reframe (the finding with the longest tail)

Searching co-op funding rather than crypto funding turned up ZAO's actual structural neighbours:

- **Ampled** - artist-owned co-op for direct fan support. Received one of six **$25,000 grants from
  AmbitioUS** made to artist cooperative organizations.
- **Resonate** - co-op music streaming, stream-to-own.
- **Groupmuse** - multistakeholder co-op for house concerts, where musicians receive 90-100% of audience
  contributions.
- **Subvert** - co-op music marketplace.
- **Platform Cooperativism Consortium** - the movement's "think-and-do tank" at The New School: a global
  network of platform co-ops, developers, artists, lawyers and **funders**. It has itself moved
  significant money (a $1M Google.org grant for open-source platform co-op tooling).

This changes who we compare ourselves to and who we talk to. In the web3 music category, The ZAO is one
of many. In the **artist-owned co-op** category, we are a well-populated 188-member organization with a
festival, a fund, live events across three continents, and working software - which is a strong entry in
a small field.

The institutional consequence: the **New Economy Coalition's Solidarity Economy Funding Library** catalogs
**350+ funders, non-extractive investors, and fiscal sponsors** moving resources into solidarity economy
projects. That is a directory of grant capital we qualify for on structure alone, entirely off-chain,
with no season, no drive, and no boost score. We have never looked at it.

**Non-extractive** is the term to learn there. It means capital that does not demand equity or growth-at-
all-costs - the exact thing that has made conventional funding a bad fit for a member-owned music org.

## One tension worth knowing (do not raise it publicly)

Artizen itself **raised $2.2M in venture capital in May 2023 from ConsenSys, Animoca Brands, and Protocol
Labs** (ZAOOS doc 683). So a fund whose entry criterion is "no VC funding", advertising itself as an
alternative to "venture-backed everything", is hosted on a venture-backed platform.

This is not hypocrisy on the Bread Fund's part and it is not a reason to skip anything. But know it before
walking into a room and monologuing about venture capital, and **do not be the one who points it out**.
Nothing is gained by making a prospective ally's host look foolish. Note it, hold it, move on.

## Recommended sequencing

The order matters more than any single item.

1. **Boost the Bread Fund and submit the four qualifying projects** (kit/bread-fund-submission-pack.md).
   This is the introduction and it costs nothing.
2. **Run one visible mobilization** for a project in that fund. Our crowd showing up is the only evidence
   that matters to a community-voted network, and it is the one thing we can demonstrate rather than
   claim.
3. **Then open the membership conversation**, leading with the culture gap and the crowd - not with a
   funding request. Reference the mobilization from step 2 as the proof.
4. **Separately and in parallel, work the off-chain rail.** Open the NEC funding library, filter for arts,
   culture and non-extractive capital, and shortlist ten. This is slower and much larger than anything on
   Artizen, and it does not compete with it for attention because different people can do it.
5. **Do not stake 10k BREAD** to buy membership. Earn it.

The through-line: we keep treating funding as campaigns to win. The higher-value pattern here is
**qualifying for recurring rails** - a monthly vote, a funder directory, a member network - where the
structure we already have does the work once, instead of a crowd being re-mobilized every drive.
