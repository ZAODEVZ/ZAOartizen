The Artizen Playbook
Thanks for joining our mission to fund human creativity.
Artizen is more than a platform. It’s a new kind of game - one designed to be fair, fun, and
rewarding for everyone who plays.

Why invent a new game? Because the old ways of funding art, science, and technology are

broken. The hoops we force creators to jump through, the impossible odds stacked against them;
it's a rigged game with dumb rules that celebrate conformity over creativity.
At Artizen, we’re flipping the board. Here, you decide what gets funded and earn a stake in the
outcome. We've raised millions for artists, entrepreneurs, scientists, and engineers. Not by
gatekeeping but by making the rules fair, the process fun, and the outcome rewarding.
Treat this like a playbook for your favorite game.
Not a list of chores but a strategy to win.

René Pinnell

 Quickstart

Artizen is a funding platform for breakthroughs in art, science, technology, and culture.

Each season:
1. People launch new Funds to support their community.
2. Mission-aligned brands and individuals sponsor Funds.
3. Creators submit projects to any Funds they qualify for.
4. Fund admins curate the projects they want to back. Every approved project also gets baseline
match from the Artizen Endowment, so no project is left without fuel.
5. Projects sell Artifacts, a new kind of digital collectible, to their fans.
6. Every $1 of sales (and every $1 of sponsorship) unlocks match funding from curated Funds and
the Endowment, scaled by that week’s Match Multiple set by Artizen.
7. The community casts Boosts on the projects and Funds they want to see win. Boosts are
powered by Boost Points, earned by donating to the Endowment or holding ART.

Each week is a Fund Drive (Thursday to Thursday):
1. Two leaderboards run in parallel: projects vs projects, Funds vs Funds.
2. Your rank is your money raised: sales + match unlocked. Prizes follow rank. Boosts win you a
share of a separate weekly Boost Bonus pot that grows as the whole community raises more
together.
3. Top ranks win weekly cash prizes from the Artizen Endowment.
4. Season-long totals roll up to a big end-of-season prize when the season closes.

 Gameplay

Whether you're a creator, community leader, or sponsor, here's how to play the game of Artizen.

 Setup
1. ENDOWMENT
The Artizen Endowment is the engine behind every cash prize and most of the match funding
Artizen puts on the table (sponsors who back Community Funds add the rest). When you
donate to it, you earn Boost Points, plus ART tokens when you donate with crypto: every $1
donated earns 100 Boost Points ($10 = 1,000, $100 = 10,000). At the start of every Fund Drive,
holding ART pays you fresh Boost Points equal to one tenth of your ART balance (ART divided
by 10, so 1,000,000 ART earns you 100,000 Boost Points), added on top of whatever points
you already have. It is cumulative: unspent points carry over, and you can keep earning and
stacking more every week. Boost Points are how you win the projects and Funds you love a
share of the weekly Boost Bonus pot. Fund the engine, get the fuel to play.
2. FUNDS
Funds back the projects that matter to a community. Two ways to play:

Launch a Fund to support creators around a cause, place, or theme you care about.
Sponsor a Fund to grow its match pool, reach its audience, earn rewards, and help it climb
the leaderboard toward cash prizes.
3. PROJECTS
Projects are the breakthroughs being funded. Two ways to play:

Submit a project to any Funds you qualify for, then sell cultural Artifacts to your fans.
Buy Artifacts from projects you love. Every purchase supports the creator, unlocks match
funding on top, and helps the project climb the leaderboard toward cash prizes.

 Curation
1. Get curated
Fund admins review submissions and curate the projects that fit. Get curated by as many
Community Funds as you can; each one adds to your available match funding. There is no
minimum: a single curation already makes your sales eligible for that Fund's match. Every
project is also auto-added to the Artizen Endowment, so you always have a baseline match to
play with.
2. Earn Boost Points
Donate to the Endowment or hold ART to earn Boost Points. Every $1 you donate to the
Endowment earns you 100 Boost Points (so $10 earns 1,000 and $100 earns 10,000). Holding
ART pays you more points at the start of each Fund Drive: one tenth of your ART balance (your
ART divided by 10) is added to your total every week, on top of whatever you already have.
Unspent points carry over, so you can keep stacking them.
3. Cast Boosts
Spend Boost Points to Boost the projects and Funds you want to see win. Boosts grow their
share of the week’s Boost Bonus pot: more boosts means a bigger share, on a deliberately
gentle curve so bonus money spreads wide.

 Competition
Every week we have a new Fund Drive (Thursday to Thursday) with two leaderboards running in
parallel:

Projects compete against other projects for the top project cash prizes.
Community Funds compete against other Funds for the top Fund cash prizes.
Both leaderboards work the same way:
1. Drive activity
Projects sell Artifacts; Funds attract sponsorships. Every $1 raised unlocks match funding,
scaled by that week's Match Multiple set by Artizen (e.g. 2× means each $1 unlocks $2 in
match), up to your available cap.
2. Rally Boosts
Ask your community to Boost your project or Boost your Fund. Every boost grows your share of
the week’s Boost Bonus pot.
3. Climb the leaderboard
Your spot is set by money raised: sales plus the match those sales unlock. The more you raise,
the higher you rank, and prizes follow rank.
4. Win weekly cash prizes
Every ranked project and Fund wins some cash, but most of the pool goes to the top. The #1 rank
takes the biggest prize, #2 gets half of #1, and #3 gets half of #2. From there the prizes flatten
out, so everyone further down the leaderboard still earns a steadier, more even share. Then the
board resets and a new drive begins.
5. Win Boost Bonuses
Boosts pay out through a separate weekly Boost Bonus pot, split among the boosted projects
and Funds. More boosts means a bigger share, on a deliberately gentle curve so bonus money
spreads wide. The pot starts at $50,000 and grows every time the whole community crosses a
fundraising milestone, so everyone wins when anyone sells. This split is a one-week experiment
starting with the Harvest Fund Drive (August 20 to 27): if it makes the game better, it stays; if
it makes the game worse, we roll back to the old way and say so.

 Example
 The Bloom Fund Drive

Theme: creativity that makes a community feel alive.
It’s a fresh weekly Fund Drive, and two races run side by side: Funds compete against Funds,
and projects compete against projects. Artizen sets this week’s Match Multiple to 3×, which
means every $1 anyone backs unlocks $3 of match funding on top.

 The Fund race
Funds are run by community leaders who curate a slate of projects. They compete for sponsor
dollars: the more a Fund raises, the bigger the match pool it can hand to its projects. Two Funds
are going head to head this week.

 Public Art Fund (Josh) rallies local businesses for $4K in sponsorships  +$12K match at
3×  $16K driven. His community Boosts the Fund hard: 8,000 boost points.
Rank: #2 on $16K raised, but those 8,000 boosts earn a hefty Boost Bonus share
 Maker Fund (Priya) raises even more, $6K in sponsorships  +$18K match  $24K driven,
but barely rallies Boosts: 300 boost points.
Rank: #1 on $24K raised, with only a sliver of the Boost Bonus
Maker Fund raised the most, so Maker Fund wins the Fund race: rank follows money raised. But
Josh’s community heat is not wasted. Those 8,000 boosts earn Public Art Fund a much bigger slice
of the week’s Boost Bonus pot, so both Funds walk away ahead.

 The Project race
Now zoom into the Public Art Fund. It curates three projects, and every sponsor dollar Josh
raised widened the match those projects can unlock, in real time. Each project sells Artifacts
(matched 3×) and rallies fans to Boost.

 Tree Sculpture (Sarah) sells $1K  +$3K match  $4K raised · 10,000 boosts
Rank: #2 on $4K raised, plus the biggest Boost Bonus share of the three
 Little Library (Zach) sells just $200  +$600 match  $800 raised, but his fans flood in:
9,000 boosts
Rank: #3 on $800 raised, but those 9,000 boosts earn a big Boost Bonus share

 Street Mural (Victor) sells the most, $1.5K  +$4.5K match  $6K raised, but barely rallies
Boosts: 100 boosts
Rank: #1 on $6K raised, with only a small Boost Bonus share
Victor sold the most, so Street Mural takes #1: rank follows dollars raised, full stop. But rank is
only half the game. Sarah’s and Zach’s boost-rich crowds earn them the biggest shares of the
week’s Boost Bonus pot, so community heat still pays real money.

 How the cash prizes pay out
Both races award cash. Every ranked project and Fund wins something, but the top of each
board earns the most: #2 gets half of #1, #3 gets half of #2, then it flattens into a steadier share
for everyone below.
In this drive’s project race the prizes land like this:
1.  Street Mural wins $2,000
2.  Tree Sculpture wins $1,000 (half of #1)
3.  Little Library wins $500 (half of #2)
Ranks 4 and below still earn cash, just on a flatter curve (think $250, $220, $200, and so on). And
prizes are only half the story: when the Boost Bonus pot pays out, Sarah and Zach collect the big
shares their crowds boosted them into, while Victor’s 100 boosts earn him just a sliver.
The Fund race pays out the same way, on its own (larger) pool:
1.  Maker Fund wins $4,000
2.  Public Art Fund wins $2,000 (half of #1)
3.  third-place Fund wins $1,000 (half of #2), and so on down the board
Here’s the compounding twist: a Fund’s cash prize doesn’t get paid out as cash, it rolls into that
Fund’s match pool for next week’s drive. Priya’s $4,000 win means the Maker Fund starts next
week with $4,000 more match to hand its projects, before she raises a single new sponsor dollar.
Win a strong week, and your projects get an even bigger head start the next.

 What carries into next week
Sponsor and sales dollars unlock match instantly for curated projects mid-drive.
A Fund’s cash prize rolls into its match pool next week (see above), so a strong week
compounds into a stronger one.
Unused match never vanishes: project leftovers return to the Fund, Fund leftovers roll into that
Fund’s next weekly drive, and any Fund balance still unallocated at season’s end stays with that
Fund and rolls over to the next season.

 The takeaway: Dollars decide rank and boosts decide bonuses. Sales and sponsorships fuel

your match and your climb up the leaderboard; Boosts win you a share of the weekly Boost Bonus
pot, and that pot grows for everyone as the whole community raises more. Get curated by as many
Funds as you can, sell Artifacts, and rally your people to Boost you. Then do it again, because every
Fund Drive is a fresh leaderboard, and this week’s wins feed next week’s match.

 Projects

We fund bold ideas in art, science, technology, and culture, at any stage, in any medium. From oil
paintings to open-source code, from raw sparks to finished works.
What we look for:

Originality. Is it fresh? Surprising? Unlike anything we’ve seen?
Impact. Will it change lives, revitalize communities, or regenerate ecosystems?
Feasibility. Can you pull it off with the team, time, and money you’ve got?
Anyone can submit, for free. But if you want to unlock match funding and win cash prizes, you’ll
need to rise above the rest. Here’s how.

 Create a Profile
To get started, head to the Artizen home page and create your account. Once you’re signed in, you
can create a project profile from your user profile page, or by clicking Submit on the home page.
Your project profile is the public face of your project, the first thing seen by your fans, friends, and
supporters. So make it dope.
REQUIRED

Logline : One-sentence hook that captures your big idea (max 140 characters)
Tags : Describe your project's focus and field
What are you making? : Say it plainly, like you’re talking to a friend (250 characters)
How will it impact the world? : Tell us why it matters (250 characters)
What progress have you made? : Show momentum (250 characters)

OPTIONAL (but powerful)

Featured Video : Link to a YouTube or Vimeo video.

 Get Funded
Once your profile’s ready, it's time to start fundraising.
1. SUBMIT TO FUNDS
Browse the active Funds and apply to every one you qualify for. From the home page you can
find them three ways: tap the search icon and enter tags or keywords, scroll down to the Funds
leaderboard to see them all, or click Submit to pick your project and pull up the full list of
open Funds (with search built in). Read each description, study the eligibility criteria, then
tailor your submission and explain why you’re a good fit. The more Funds you submit to, the
more shots you have at getting approved.
2. GET APPROVED
Fund admins review every submission and decide whether your project fits their eligibility
criteria. Community support plays a big role here. Boosts and engagement signal how deep and
broad your following is and help your project get full consideration. But they don’t autoapprove you. Final decisions always rest with Fund Directors and admins, who approve or
remove projects based on fit with their mission.
3. RAISE MONEY
Once you’re approved, you’re in the game. Start selling Artifacts to your fans. Every $1 you raise
unlocks match funding from each Fund backing your project, at that week’s Match Multiple,
while the Fund’s match lasts. The more you sell, the more match you unlock, and the higher you
climb the leaderboard. Top-ranked projects also win weekly cash prizes.

 Fund Drives
Every week, Artizen runs a Fund Drive: a fresh, time-limited competition to unlock match funding
and win cash prizes. Each drive starts a new leaderboard, so every week is a clean shot at winning,
no matter how last week went.

 Your available match funding (your goal for the week)
At the start of every Fund Drive, your project is given a pool of available match funding. This is
the number to beat. It comes from two places: the Artizen Endowment (set by that week’s Match
Multiple) and every Fund that has curated you.
Each Fund you’re curated for contributes a slice of its own available match to you. The percentage
you’re allocated from a curated Fund is based on your sales over the last 4 weeks compared to the
other projects in that Fund: more recent sales than the other projects means a larger match
allocation. Each Fund also has a floor and ceiling percentage, which sets the minimum and
maximum a single project can receive that week. The floor and ceiling are based on the number of
curated projects in the Fund. All allocations are calculated so that no more than 100% of a Fund is
handed out in any given week. So getting curated by more Funds, and staying active with recent
sales, both deepen the pool of match you can unlock.
That whole pool is reserved for you for the entire drive. Nobody else can take it. The number you
see on your project’s Fund Drive card is exactly what your community needs to unlock before the
clock runs out.

 How you unlock it
Match unlocks as your fans buy Artifacts, at that week’s Match Multiple. At a 3× multiple, every $1
a fan spends unlocks $3 of your available match, for $4 total raised on that dollar, until your pool
runs out.
Whatever available match you don’t unlock by the end of the drive is returned to the Endowment
and the curated Funds. It isn’t lost forever: it flows back into the pots that fund next week. Your
next drive then opens with a freshly calculated amount of available match, which may be more or
less than this week’s, depending on how much money is left and how well you performed.

 Rank, boosts, and the Boost Bonus
Your community also casts Boost Points for your project to grow its share of the week’s Boost
Bonus pot. (You and your supporters earn Boost Points in lots of ways: holding ART, donating to
the Artizen Endowment, completing your user profile, attending Artizen events, and other
prosocial activities that add value to the community.)
Your rank is set by money raised: sales + match unlocked. The more you raise, the higher you
climb, and cash prizes follow rank. Boosts pay a different way: every drive has a Boost Bonus pot,
and the more boosts your project earns, the bigger your share of it, on a deliberately gentle curve
so bonus money spreads wide. Your share shifts live all week and locks in when the drive closes,
just like prizes. The pot also grows for everyone as the whole community raises more together: it
starts at $50,000 and climbs with collective milestones (at $500K raised it becomes $100,000, at
$1M it becomes $150,000, at $1.5M it becomes $200,000, and at $2M it becomes $250,000). So
every sale, yours or anyone’s, makes the whole week richer.
We are running this split as a one-week experiment, starting with the Harvest Fund Drive (August
20 to 27). If it makes the game better, it stays. If it makes the game worse, we roll back to the old
way and say so.

 Cash prizes
When the drive ends, cash prizes are awarded by rank. Every ranked project wins something, but
most of the pool goes to the top: the #1 project takes the biggest prize, #2 earns half of #1, and #3
earns half of #2, then the rest flatten into a steadier share. Your prize is added to your project’s
total money raised at the end of the drive. For a full worked example, see the Gameplay section.

 Your Fund Drive card
The Fund Drive card on your project page is scoped to your project only. When someone boosts or
buys on that card, they’re contributing to you. Want to see the math behind it? Tap the Fund Drive
title on the card and a popup breaks down the specifics: your available match, what’s been
unlocked, and your current rank.

 Timing and the fair finish
Drives end Thursdays at 11:00 AM PST. The next Fund Drive begins as close to immediately
after as possible, so the rhythm is weekly and continuous.

Fair finish. A drive doesn’t end the instant the clock hits zero. At the scheduled close, a fresh 5minute timer starts for the #1 project. If that project holds first place for the full 5 minutes, the
drive closes and winners are final. The timer restarts only if a different project takes #1; sales
that do not flip the leader do not extend the race.

 Strategy
Boost early and often. Boosts grow your share of the week’s Boost Bonus pot, and shares shift
live until the drive closes, so keep the heat coming.

Raise all week. Every sale unlocks match and lifts your rank. Steady selling beats a single lastminute push.

Have your community ready for the finish. Because of the 5-minute fair finish, the very end is
where ranks are won and lost. Line up your supporters so they can buy on cue when it matters
most.

Get curated by more Funds. More Funds backing you means a deeper pool of available match
to unlock in a single drive.
Want to grow faster? Check out Quests in our grow app: mini-games that reward you for growing
the whole ecosystem.

 Payouts
How and when you get paid.
Each Artifact costs $10 (plus applicable fees at checkout). Artifact sales count toward your season
payout. After the season ends, we calculate final totals (sales + match awards + prizes), complete
reviews, and then issue payouts.
Payouts are issued after the season ends so we can finalize results, confirm cleared transactions,
and process payouts accurately. It also keeps the game fair. If money went out mid-season, a bad
actor could spin up a project, buy their own Artifacts to unlock match funding and win cash prizes,
collect the payout, then round-trip that same money back into the next Fund Drive. Settling once
at the end of the season closes that loop.
How much a $1 sale unlocks depends on the week’s Match Multiple, while money lasts. At a 3×
multiple, a $1 sale unlocks $3 in match, for $4 total raised. That match is pulled from all the Funds
you’re curated for, in proportion to each Fund’s size, so getting curated by more Funds simply gives
you a deeper pool of available match. Match awards are tracked throughout the season and
included in your final payout. Prize money is added to your project’s total money raised at the
end of each weekly Fund Drive, based on your final rank that week. The only exception is the
season’s final week: instead of starting fresh, every project gets a head start. Your total season
sales and total match unlocked are converted to boost points at the standard rate of $1 = 100
boost points and added to your boost points received at the start of the Finale, so everything you
earned all season counts toward the final ranking.
At the end of the season, you’ll receive one combined payout:

Artifact Sales +
Match Funding +
Any Cash Prizes
After a season ends, payouts are processed in batches following a review and audit period. Most
creators are paid within a few weeks, though some payouts may take longer depending on size,
review requirements, and payment clearing timelines. Artizen collects all fees upfront when
Artifacts are purchased or Funds are sponsored, so there are no additional deductions at payout
beyond any actual transfer costs. If you would rather not wait, you can take your Artifact sales and
close out at any time with an Early Exit.

Know Your Creator. Banks call it Know Your Customer; we take it further. Every payout is Artizen

vouching that the creator and the project are real. Beyond standard identity and compliance
checks, our review gets to know you and your work, and may include examples of past projects, a
short video call, or a reference from someone in the Artizen network. Review begins as soon as you
submit your payout form. Smaller payouts go first, and most payouts clear within 60 days of active
review. Larger payouts (above $10,000) and any that need deeper verification receive enhanced
review and can take longer; if yours will, we tell you before day 60. The clock runs only while the
payout is in our hands and your Impact Report is complete, and it pauses whenever we’re waiting
on you, like a form, a confirmation, a test payment, or your Impact Report materials. If you would
rather not wait, the door is always open: you can take your Artifact sales and close out at any time
with an Early Exit.

Your Impact Report. Every funded project completes an Impact Report before its payout

completes. It is how we verify your work and how we celebrate it, in the same motion. We fund
creators with money entrusted to us by the backers of our endowment, and the Impact Report is
how we show them, and the world, that every dollar went to a real creator building a real project.
Your report comes together in the Impact Report editor on your payout page and includes:

Links to your work, live and public
Images of the project (at least 5)
A recorded interview: answer four simple questions in your own voice. Record it on camera,
record audio only, or upload a recording you already have. Phone quality is exactly right.

People who know the work (at least two, with contact details)
A short narrative of the progress you made this season
Here’s the fun part: everything you share feeds a feature essay about your work. With your
permission, we publish your story to our Substack and feature it across Artizen’s emails and
marketing, reaching the whole Artizen audience. The report is part of every payout; publishing it is
always your choice.
Your payout review runs in parallel, so the report never has to slow anything down. If a project
can’t be verified with the materials provided, your Artifact sales are still returned to you promptly,
but match funding, prizes, and award money are not released. That’s the deal that keeps every
payout trustworthy, for creators, for fans, and for the backers whose money funds the match.
Returning creators complete a fresh Impact Report each season they’re funded, showing the
progress made since the last one. Season over season, your reports become a public track record of
your work, and that track record is worth more than any single payout.
Artifact sales, match awards, and your weekly Fund Drive prizes are all paid in US dollars to a
bank account, or in USDC to your Artizen wallet. Weekly prize money is simply added to your
project’s total money raised. The one exception is the season’s final overall prize, awarded in the
last week: that is paid in ART, the Artizen token, giving creators an ownership stake in the
platform.

 Tips for Success
How to earn more boosts, sell more Artifacts, and build lasting support:

Direct asks
Reach out directly to friends, fans, and collaborators with a short message and your project
link. Boosting is free. Buying an Artifact is $10. Both take less than a minute.

Post on social
Share updates and milestones. Behind-the-scenes posts work well. Tag @Artizen and we often
repost.

Respond to feedback
Read every comment. If it’s helpful, revise your profile. This shows you're collaborative and
serious.

Use every channel
Promote your project via email, DMs, social, livestreams, Discord, or anywhere your people
hang out.

Share progress
Every milestone builds trust. Trust drives boosts, sales, and long-term support.

 Rewards for Fans
Once published, you have the option to add Rewards for fans who back your project.
By default, fans get:

Artifacts : 1 Artifact for every $10 they spend
Impact : Every $1 unlocks match funding and cash prizes
But the magic happens when you offer rewards of your own. Just like Kickstarter, you can offer
perks based on how much fans give. Digital downloads. Merch. Zoom hangs. VIP access. Behindthe-scenes drops. Whatever makes sense for your project and audience.
BEST PRACTICES

Keep it real : Only offer what you can deliver.
Make it special : Give fans something they can’t get anywhere else.
Price smart : Balance reward value with what you need to raise.
Use tiers : Offer multiple reward tiers to match different budgets.
Great image : Keep it simple and clean. It should look great even at small sizes.
While you can add as many rewards as you want, we recommend 3 to 5. Enough to give people
options without overwhelming them.

 Funds

Launch a Fund. Grow your community.
A Fund on Artizen is simply a pool of capital to support creators. Anyone can launch a Fund, and
we make it easy to raise money and curate the best projects.
Here’s the magic: every $1 raised from sponsors unlocks match funding from our Endowment
and helps your Fund compete and win cash prizes. All of it is pooled and made available to support
the projects you curate.
It’s the best way to amplify your impact for the creators you love.

Important legal note: Funds on Artizen are not investment vehicles or securities. Sponsorships
are not investments and do not provide equity, profit rights, or financial returns. Artizen has
consulted outside legal counsel who has reviewed this structure and confirmed that Funds are
community funding programs, not regulated investment products.

 Apply to Launch
We look for passionate leaders rooted in creative communities, from basket weaving to
biohacking. If approved, we kickstart your Fund with at least a $1,000 contribution.
Your Fund must include:

Fund Title: Short, clear, and focused.
Logline: One sentence that captures your mission and who you support.
Tags: Keywords that define your focus. (e.g., “Afrofuturism,” “Climate Tech,” “Agentic AI”)
Description: A short paragraph that explains what your community stands for. What’s your
mission? Why does it matter?

Eligibility Requirements: Who qualifies for support? Be specific. (e.g., “Projects led by women
building open-source tech”)

Hero Image: One strong 16:9 image that sets the vibe. No text or graphic overlays. The app
layers its own UI elements, including text, over your hero image, and crops vary across cards
and screens, so baked-in lettering collides or gets sliced. Save text-heavy designs for your
Fund's PDF upload.
Launch New Fund

Directing a Fund means creators depend on you, so Directors and curators are held to the higher
conduct bar in our Code of Conduct.

 Curate Projects
Curate the best projects with your community.
1. Define Your Focus
Start by defining clear eligibility requirements. What kind of creators or projects do you want
to support? Be specific and intentional.
2. Let Your Community Boost
All projects receive boosts from fans and the community. Boosts are a powerful signal: they
show the depth and breadth of support and help you prioritize which projects deserve your full
attention.
3. Approve Best Submissions
Approve projects that meet your criteria. If one doesn’t fit, remove it with a short note so
creators can revise and resubmit. This isn’t about judging quality, just eligibility. One note on
the numbers: the count on your Fund’s Curation tab includes projects you have already
removed as well as the ones still waiting for review, so it can read higher than your actual
pending queue.
It’s a simple system. You ensure every project fits your mission.

Review within 2 to 3 weeks. We ask every Fund to review the submissions it receives within 2 to

Funding on Artizen happens through Fund Drives: weekly, time-limited competitions. Each drive

 Unlock match funding for your Fund
At the start of every drive, Artizen calculates how much available match funding your Fund has
access to. The more sponsor money your Fund has been bringing in, the more match the
Endowment makes available to you. Specifically, we look at your Fund’s trailing 4-week average
of sponsor contributions relative to every other Fund: the more active your Fund has been at
getting sponsors on board, the larger your share of available match funding. So your first job each

available match runs dry.
Treat that available match as your goal for the week: unlock all of it. Bring in enough sponsors
that, at the current Match Multiple, every dollar of available match from the Endowment gets
unlocked. That is how you grow your Fund and create the biggest impact for the creators you care
about.

 Win cash prizes for your Fund
Funds compete against other Funds on a single global Funds leaderboard. Your Fund climbs it with
money raised: the sponsorships you bring in plus the match they unlock. The higher your Fund
ranks, the bigger the cash prize it wins. We use a halving-style curve, so the top Funds earn the
most, but every ranked Fund gets paid. Boosts pay Funds a different way: the boosts your
community casts on your Fund win it a share of the week’s Boost Bonus pot, spread wide on
purpose so more Funds walk away with something. A Fund’s prize doesn’t pay out as cash to you;
it rolls into your Fund’s match pool for the next drive, deepening the support you can offer
creators the following week. Win, and you have more to give.

after as possible, so the rhythm is weekly and continuous.

Fair finish. At the scheduled close, a fresh 5-minute timer starts for the #1 spot. If the leader
holds for the full 5 minutes, the drive closes and winners are final. The timer restarts only if a
different Fund takes #1; activity that does not flip the leader does not extend the race. Rally
your community to be ready for the finish.

Artifacts cost $10 and are sold as open edition NFTs throughout the season. You can buy as many

One for the Creator

 Investing in Impact
Artifacts aren’t equity. They don’t give you shares, control, or IP rights in a project. With Artifacts,
you don’t own the project - you own the impact.
Like all works of art, the value of an Artifact comes from its aesthetics, rarity, and emotional
resonance. But its true worth depends on the impact of the project it represents. The greater the

impact, the more valuable the Artifact.

For example, imagine a research project in material science. Their Artifact might simply be a math
equation scribbled in a notebook. But if that equation changes how we produces energy, its value
could skyrocket.
Most Artifacts will go to zero - because most projects fail. But a few will transform the world.
And those will be priceless.

 How ART Works
The Endowment runs on a Revnet, an autonomous financial engine built on Juicebox. Think of it
less like a slot machine and more like a vending machine you can trust: put money in, and you
know exactly what comes out. The rules are immutable, on-chain, and fully autonomous. No secret
levers.
1. Dollars in, ART out. The Revnet mints new ART or buys it on the market, whichever is cheaper.
Your dollars stay locked in the Revnet.
2. Every contribution splits three ways. 60% to buyers as ART, 10% to the Artizen team, and 30%
into the Endowment, where it is pooled and awarded to creators.
3. Revenue fuels buybacks. Platform revenue and Treasury funds are used to buy back ART,
adding steady demand independent of speculation.
4. Price doubles on a schedule. The mint price doubles on a fixed timeline, forever, rewarding
early supporters and creating scarcity over time.
5. Cash-out guarantee. Burn ART to redeem US dollars from the Revnet, or sell on the open
market.
6. A floor that only climbs. A 10% exit fee on redemptions pushes the floor price higher each
time. Flippers pay the fee; long-term holders win.
The result is a feedback loop: the more ART we sell, the bigger the Endowment grows; the bigger
the Endowment, the more creators we fund; the more creators we fund, the more Artifacts they
sell; the more sales, the more revenue for buybacks. ART trades between a rising floor and an
exponential ceiling.
It is grounded in reality, not hype. The dollars are locked in USDC and redeemable. Each token is
backed by the Endowment’s treasury and our growing collection of Artifacts. The Revnet is built
on Juicebox, the same proven infrastructure that has processed over $200 million for more than a
thousand community treasuries. Like all crypto systems, this is still experimental and carries risk.

 FAQ

Learning the rules to any new game can be confusing. We get it. Artizen mixes elements of
crowdfunding, crypto, and community competition, so of course there are a lot of moving parts.
No question is dumb. If you’re unsure about something, you’re not alone. This FAQ clears up the
most common points of confusion so you can focus on playing and winning the game.

 Artifacts
What exactly is an Artifact?
An Artifact is a digital collectible that represents a project. Each sale supports the creator, unlocks
match funding and prizes, and becomes part of a fixed edition at season’s end.

If I buy an Artifact, do I own the project?
No. Artifacts don’t give you control, equity, or rights. You own the collectible itself. Its value comes
from the project’s cultural or social impact, not financial claims.

Why would I buy more than one Artifact?
More Artifacts mean more rewards and more direct support for the creator. Plus, if the project
succeeds, your Artifacts could appreciate in value and become culturally important collectibles.
Think of Andy Warhol’s silkscreen prints. At the time they were just multiples, but as his work
reshaped the art world, those prints skyrocketed in value. If a project on Artizen changes the
world of art, science, technology, or culture, you’ll wish you held more than one.

Why should fans spend $10 on an Artifact that might be worthless?
Most Artifacts will go to zero. That’s honest. But a few projects will change the world. Those
Artifacts will be priceless. Fans buy both for the upside and to support projects they believe in. The
$10 isn’t charity, it’s skin in the game.

Are Artifacts regulated as securities?
No. Artifacts are unique works of art, open-edition NFTs under the hood. Art is the last great
unregulated market. Anyone can buy and sell art. That’s why Artifacts are digital collectibles, not
equity, share, stocks, or securities.

 Projects
How does match funding work?
Every Fund Drive, Artizen sets a Match Multiple for each Fund. When you sell an Artifact, your
sales are matched at that multiple from the Funds that have approved your project, up to the
match available that week. Get approved by more Funds, and sell while match is still available, to
unlock more. There is no minimum number of Funds your project needs: a single active Fund's
curation already makes your sales eligible for that Fund's match, and every project also has
baseline match from the Endowment. The “5 curated projects” minimum you may see elsewhere in
this Playbook is a rule for Funds becoming active, never a requirement on projects.

How is prize money different from match funding?
Match funding is tied directly to sales: you sell, Artizen matches it at the Fund Drive’s Match
Multiple. Prize money is different. It is cash from the Endowment, awarded each Fund Drive based
on your rank, which is set by how much you raised (sales plus match unlocked). Boosts pay out
separately, through the weekly Boost Bonus pot: the more boosts your project earns, the bigger its
share, on a deliberately gentle curve that spreads bonus money wide. Every Fund Drive there are
two leaderboards: one ranking projects against projects, and one ranking Funds against Funds. The
top performers on each win the biggest prizes.

How do I read my project’s Total Raised?
Tap the Total Raised number on your project page and a popup breaks it down for you. If your
project has competed across multiple seasons, you’ll see one line per season plus the all-time total.
Each season’s number is the sum of three things:

Artifact sales: every dollar fans spent collecting your Artifact
Match unlocked: the matching dollars your sales unlocked from the funds that curated you
Prizes won: prize money you earned from weekly drives
Prize money is added when each weekly drive closes. During a live drive, your current raised
amount updates in real time, and your leaderboard rank (and the prize it would pay) can shift all
week. So a mid-week prize number is a projection, not a promise. The final amount locks in when
the drive ends.
If the popup number ever looks off, ask Venus. She pulls the live breakdown straight from the
platform.

How and when do creators actually get paid?
Payouts go out after each season ends, so all sales, match, and prizes can be audited and paid
together. It normally takes a few weeks for payouts to start; smaller payouts go first, and most
clear within 60 days of active review, the time your payout is in our hands with your Impact
Report complete. Payouts above $10,000, or any that need deeper verification, receive enhanced
review and can take longer, and if yours will, we tell you before day 60. The clock pauses whenever
we’re waiting on you, and if you would rather not wait, the door is always open: you can take your
Artifact sales and close out at any time with an Early Exit. If Artizen has elected Early Exit, the
normal season calendar and combined payout do not apply. See Platform Continuity.
You choose how you get paid: US dollars to a bank account, or USDC (a U.S. dollar stablecoin) to a
crypto wallet. Artifact sales, match funding, and weekly Fund Drive cash prizes are all paid this
way. The one exception is the season finale prize, which is paid in ART, Artizen’s token.

Can creators buy Artifacts from their own project?

Yes. Creators are allowed to support their own projects. This is common when creators use
personal funds or grants that can’t be routed directly through Artizen.
To keep the game fair, all sales, match funding, and prizes are held until the season ends, so
creators cannot recycle funds or artificially amplify results mid-season.

Who decides which projects get curated?
Each Fund is run by its Fund Director and a team of curators. They review every submission and
approve the projects that meet their eligibility criteria and fit the spirit of their Fund.
Boosts matter: they signal how much real support a project has and help curators prioritize what
to review, but boosts do not auto-approve anyone. Final decisions always rest with the Fund’s
Director and curators.

What if a creator raises money but doesn’t deliver?
That risk is real. Creators keep their sales, but must use awards to advance their project. We expect
updates, and projects can be removed if they misrepresent themselves. Failure is part of discovery.
Markets reward what works and punish what doesn’t. That’s how progress happens.

What if a creator takes the money and disappears?
It will happen. Risk is real. Our rules require creators to use awards to advance their projects, but
rules alone can’t stop bad actors. Scammers gonna scam. The best guard against this is reputation.
Creators who don’t deliver will be booted from the platform, and their chances of raising again will
vanish. Over time, the market weeds out bad faith players and rewards those who consistently
build and deliver.

 Payouts
Why do payouts happen after the season ends?
Payouts happen after the season ends to keep the game fair. If creators could receive funds during
a season, they could recycle that money back into their own project to unlock more match funding
or climb the leaderboard, creating an unfair feedback loop. Holding funds until the season ends
prevents this kind of self-reinforcing behavior and ensures that all match funding and prizes are
earned through real, external support.
Delaying payouts also allows us to finalize rankings, confirm Artifact sales and match funding
across Funds, and ensure that all payments have fully cleared before money is distributed.
Together, these safeguards protect creators, sponsors, and the integrity of the competition and
makes traditional wash sales and self-reinforcing loops impossible during a season.

How long will it take to receive my payout?
Payouts begin shortly after each season ends and are processed in batches. Most creators receive
their payout within a few weeks. Smaller payouts are prioritized and tend to clear first. Most
payouts clear within 60 days of active review, the days your payout is actually in our hands with
your Impact Report complete. Larger payouts (above $10,000) and any that need deeper
verification receive enhanced review and can take longer; if yours will, we tell you before day 60.
When we’re waiting on you, for example to confirm a test payment, share missing info, or send
your Impact Report materials, the clock pauses and resumes as soon as you respond. Our goal is
always to beat that window, and most payouts do. If you would rather not wait, the door is always
open: you can take your Artifact sales and close out at any time with an Early Exit.
Every payout also goes through our Know Your Creator review. Beyond standard identity and
compliance checks, we take the time to know you and your work, which may include examples of
past projects, a short video call, or a reference from someone in the Artizen network. It’s how we
make sure every dollar Artizen pays out goes to a real creator building a real project.
Before releasing funds, we finalize season results, confirm Artifact sales and match funding, ensure
leaderboard rankings and prize allocations are correct, and verify that all credit card, bank
transfers, and other payments have fully cleared. We also complete required tax and compliance
checks so payouts are issued correctly and lawfully.
Our goal is to pay creators as quickly as possible while maintaining a fair and trusted system for
everyone. If we need anything from you to complete your payout, we’ll reach out directly.
Otherwise, you’ll receive a confirmation as soon as your payout is on the way.

What is an Impact Report?
It’s the story of what you built, and the final step of every payout. Before a funded project’s payout
completes, the creator shares links to the work, at least five images, a short recorded interview
answering four simple questions in their own voice, a couple of people who know the work, and a
narrative of the season’s progress. We verify it, then we celebrate it: every report feeds a feature
essay, published to our Substack with the creator’s permission. Artizen funds projects with money
entrusted to us by the backers of our endowment, and Impact Reports are how we show that
every dollar went to a real creator building a real project. If a project can’t be verified, Artifact sales
are returned promptly, but match funding, prizes, and award money are not released. Returning
creators complete a new report each season, building a public track record of real progress.

Why do some creators get paid earlier than others?
Payouts are processed in batches, and different payouts require different levels of review. Smaller
payouts are usually faster to verify and can often be processed sooner. Larger payouts may require
additional review, compliance checks, or clearing time, and in some cases may be handled
separately. The goal is to pay everyone as quickly as possible while keeping the process accurate
and compliant.

What do I need to provide in order to get paid?
Before we can release a payout, we need you to confirm who is legally receiving the funds and
provide the appropriate tax documentation. We may also ask you to confirm basic compliance
acknowledgments required to process payments safely and lawfully. If anything is missing or
doesn’t match, we’ll follow up, and your payout may be delayed until it’s resolved.

How does Artizen handle payment compliance and wallet screening?
Artizen applies a risk-based compliance approach similar to other online creator marketplaces. For
traditional payments, we rely on Stripe’s payment infrastructure, which performs fraud
prevention, sanctions screening, and payment compliance. For creator payouts, Artizen performs
identity verification (KYC), AML checks, and tax compliance before funds are disbursed. Crypto
payments are screened against known prohibited wallets, and we are expanding our blockchain
risk screening as the platform grows. We also reserve the right to reject, freeze, or investigate
transactions when suspicious activity is identified or when required by law.

Are payouts taxable?
Yes. In most cases, payouts are considered taxable income. Artizen does not provide tax advice,
and you are responsible for determining how to report these funds based on your local laws and
circumstances. We may be required to collect tax forms and issue tax reporting documents
depending on your jurisdiction and the type of recipient.

How are payouts issued, and what’s the difference between USDC and ART?
Artifact sales, match funding, and weekly Fund Drive cash prizes are all paid in US dollars, either to
a bank account or as USDC (a U.S. dollar stablecoin) to a crypto wallet. The creator decides which.
The only payout in ART is the season finale prize. ART is Artizen’s native token and represents a
stake in the Artizen Endowment and platform. Some winners hold their ART long-term if they
believe Artizen will keep growing; others redeem or sell some for liquidity. What you do with ART
is up to you.

Why is the season finale prize paid in ART?
To be clear on what’s paid how: all weekly Fund Drive money, sales, match, and cash prizes, is US
dollar denominated. Only the season finale prize is paid in ART. We pay the finale in ART because it
aligns the season’s top creators with the long-term growth of the Endowment. Instead of a onetime cash payout, ART gives them a stake in the system that funds future seasons, with the option
to participate in the upside as Artizen grows.

 Funds
Can anyone launch a Fund?
Yes, if approved. Artizen is looking for passionate leaders embedded in creative communities.
Maybe you organize underground film screenings, host symposiums on emerging tech, or lead a
thriving Discord for AI artists. Perhaps you’ve built a space, physical or digital, where artists
connect, scientists collaborate, or coders hack.
What matters is that you have a clear vision for your Fund and the drive to bring together creators,
fans, and sponsors. If approved, we kickstart your Fund with $1,000, and you’ll earn rewards like a
bonus in ART based on sponsorship money raised.
Ready? Apply to launch a Fund.

What’s the role of Fund Directors?
The Fund Director owns the Fund. They launch it, set its eligibility rules, and recruit a team of
curators to help run it, so most Funds have several curators working alongside the Director.
Together they review submissions and approve or remove projects based on the Fund’s rules. Their
job isn’t to judge whether a project is “good” or “bad,” only whether it meets the stated
requirements and the spirit of the Fund. The full Artizen community boosts projects to signal
support and drive competition, but the Director and curators always retain final authority over
approvals and removals. They do not: provide investment advice, act as fiduciaries, manage capital
for profit, or have any duty to maximize financial returns.

Are Funds an investment product?
No. Funds are not investment vehicles. Sponsorships are contributions to support creative
projects, not investments.
Sponsors receive no equity, profit rights, or financial returns.
Artizen has consulted outside legal counsel who has reviewed and confirmed this structure.

Can my contribution be tax-deductible?
Contributions made directly to Artizen Works, Inc. (a Delaware C-Corp doing business as Artizen)
are not tax-deductible charitable donations. Many sponsors treat them as a business or marketing
expense instead; ask your accountant what fits your situation.
If you need a charitable tax deduction, Artizen has a fiscal sponsor: SpiritDAO, a 501(c)(3)
nonprofit. Contribute through SpiritDAO’s fiscal sponsorship page and your donation supports the
Fund or project of your choice, with a deduction attached. One critical rule: the money must flow
through SpiritDAO directly from the start. A contribution made on the Artizen platform cannot be
claimed as tax-deductible after the fact. It’s a manual process, so allow extra time, and reach out to
hello@artizen.fund if you’d like a hand setting it up.

How are Fund prizes calculated?
The top three ranks are weighted heavily. On both the project and the Fund leaderboards, each of
the top three prizes is double the size of the rank below it: first place is double second, second is
double third. From rank four down to the last ranked spot, prizes step down more gently, on a
linear curve, so every project that ranks still earns a meaningful cut. The goal is to reward the very
best while making sure no one who ranks walks away empty-handed.

What happens to match funding that is not awarded?
Unused match doesn’t disappear. Within a Fund Drive, match a project doesn’t use flows to the
rest of that Fund’s projects; match a Fund doesn’t use rolls into that Fund’s next weekly drive; and
anything still left at season’s end stays with that Fund and rolls over to the next season. Nothing
returns to the Endowment at season close.

 ART Token
How do I get ART, and how does it relate to the Endowment?
Go to the Artizen home page and donate to the Endowment with a crypto payment (your Artizen
wallet is the easiest way). Crypto donations earn you ART and Boost Points; card and bank
donations earn Boost Points only. Every $1 you donate earns 100 Boost Points (so $10 = 1,000 and
$100 = 10,000), and holding ART pays you more Boost Points (your ART balance divided by 10) at
the start of every Fund Drive, added to your total. Backing the Endowment and owning a stake in
it are the same action. Under the hood, ART runs on a Revnet: your dollars are locked on-chain, you
receive ART, and a share of every contribution flows into the Endowment that funds creators. ART
is redeemable for US dollars at a floor price that rises over time. See the Endowment section for
the full mechanics.

How do ART holders earn Boost Points each week?
At the start of each Fund Drive, you are paid Boost Points equal to your ART balance divided by 10:
1,293,169 ART earns 129,317 Boost Points. There is no price lookup and no dollar conversion
involved; the formula is flat. The payment is cumulative: it is added to whatever Boost Points you
already have, whether that is zero or a million. Unspent points carry over from drive to drive and
nothing is ever taken away, so you can keep earning and stacking points for as long as you hold
ART.

What makes the ART token valuable?
ART’s value, if any, comes from how useful it is in the Artizen ecosystem and how the broader
market prices it. Like all crypto assets, it can be volatile and may go to zero. We share mechanics
and on-chain data for transparency, but nothing here is a promise of returns.

How is ART different from other tokens?
ART is designed around transparent on-chain mechanics and ties closely to the Artizen ecosystem.
It’s experimental, and it carries the same risks as other tokens: volatility, regulatory uncertainty,
and the possibility of total loss.

Isn’t the ART token just another crypto scam?
ART is implemented with smart contracts that make key mechanics transparent on-chain. That
said, risk is real. ART could fail, and you should treat it as highly speculative. Only participate if you
understand the risks.

Could regulators classify the ART token as a security?
Possibly. Crypto regulation is evolving, and ART may be treated differently across jurisdictions.
We’re building in good faith and will adapt as laws and guidance change.

 Platform
How does Artizen make money?
We add a 10% fee on top of both Artifact sales and sponsorship sales. These fees are collected
upfront at the point of purchase and flow directly into the Artizen Endowment, which awards
match funding and prizes for creators. We also earn 10% of every ART token purchase.
This mix of sales fees and ART revenue funds the platform. More importantly, it aligns our success
with yours: the healthier the Endowment and the more creators thrive, the stronger Artizen
becomes.

Isn’t this just Kickstarter with extra steps?
No. Kickstarter is a one-off tool to collect money from your own network. Unless you go viral,
that’s the limit. Artizen takes that same effort and amplifies it. Every dollar you raise from a fan is
matched by the Funds that approved you, at a Match Multiple Artizen sets each week. On top of
that, the top projects each Fund Drive win cash prizes from the Endowment.

What happens if nobody buys ART or Artifacts?
Then creators don’t raise much. Simple as that. Markets don’t reward ideas no one supports. But
the model scales with demand: more buyers  bigger Endowment  bigger prizes  more
creators.

Why would brands sponsor a Fund instead of just running their own grant program?
Because Artizen is cheaper and more efficient. Brands don’t need to manage applications, reviews,
or payouts. The community curates projects, and match funding multiplies their money. They get
more impact for less overhead.

What stops big creators from dominating every season?
By “big creators,” we mean those with large fanbases or access to capital. Artizen has several
safeguards to keep them from swamping the system:

Boost Points cost more the more you use. Everyone gets a voice, but not all voices are equally
loud. Boosts are spent from a balance, and each additional boost on the same project costs
more, so raw spending power hits diminishing returns. Boosts must be cast manually too,
forcing people to invest their time as well as their influence.

Fund Director oversight. Even if a creator has a massive audience, their project must still meet
the Fund’s eligibility rules. Fund Directors can remove submissions that don’t fit the spirit or
requirements of their Fund.

Match is capped each week. Match for a Fund Drive is limited and shared across that Fund’s
projects, so no single project can drain the whole pool, even with a big wallet. A wealthy
creator can’t simply buy up their own Artifacts (or have rich friends do it) to swallow the
match. Once the week’s match is used, there is no more to unlock.

Bonuses spread wide by design. Boosts feed a separate Boost Bonus pot with a deliberately
gentle curve, so piling boosts onto one project hits fast diminishing returns and bonus money
reaches deep into the leaderboard.

How does Artizen prevent fake boosting and manipulation?
It’s a real challenge, but we’ve built in safeguards:

Phone verification. To boost, you must verify a phone number. That makes mass fake accounts
harder to spin up.

Limited free boosts. New users get only a small batch of free boosts, which run out quickly. To
gain more, you need to contribute money or succeed as a creator.

Rising boost cost. Each extra boost on a project costs more, so even “whales” face diminishing
returns. And every boost has to be clicked manually, forcing people to spend time, not just
money.
Together, these checks make it costly and time-consuming to cheat, while rewarding real
engagement and community support.

What’s to stop Artizen from changing the rules whenever it wants?
Some rules can’t change at all. The ART token runs on immutable smart contracts: issuance, floor
price, and seller fees are hardwired. No one, including us, can rewrite those rules.
Other policies, like how Funds operate, how boosts are counted, or how prizes are distributed, can
evolve. But we don’t flip switches overnight. Changes are usually discussed for a long time and
shaped by community feedback. The goal is to refine the system, not surprise people.
Long term, our aim is to move more and more of Artizen onto smart contracts, pieces of code that
are autonomous and unchangeable. But we’ll only do that once we’re very confident a mechanism
is ready to be frozen forever. This is the standard progressive decentralization approach: start
flexible while we learn, then harden rules in code once they prove themselves.
So yes, some policies can shift, but always with the mission in mind, and always moving toward
greater immutability over time.

Why should anyone believe this will last?
Because it’s built like Bitcoin: transparent rules, long-term incentives, and an engine that’s
designed to sustain itself. Every season grows the Endowment, every sale adds Artifacts to the
treasury, and every prize awarded to creators fuels demand. The system compounds over time.

What is a Fund Drive?
A Fund Drive is Artizen’s weekly competition. Each one runs Friday through Thursday. Projects
compete against other projects, and Funds compete against other Funds, on two leaderboards. At
the close, the top performers win cash prizes from the Endowment based on their rank: the
money they raised plus the match it unlocked. Boosted projects and Funds also collect shares of
the week’s Boost Bonus pot. Then a new drive begins with a fresh leaderboard and a fresh shot at
the top. Your unspent Boost Points carry over: they are yours until you spend them.

When does Season 7 end?
Season 7 ends around January 7, 2027. We always close each season with a fun event, so the exact
date may shift a week or two in either direction. All sales, match funding, and prizes are finalized
when the season closes, and payouts follow. If Artizen has elected Early Exit, the normal season
calendar and combined payout do not apply. See Platform Continuity.

What happens if someone in the community harasses me?
Tell us: support@artizen.fund. We review every report, keep your identity confidential, and
respond in proportion to what happened, up to and including removal from Artizen. See our Code
of Conduct and Enforcement & Removal policies.

 Boosts
What are Boosts?
Boosts are how the community shows support. You spend Boost Points on the projects you believe
in, and a project’s boosts win it a share of that week’s Boost Bonus pot. The more genuine support
a project earns, the bigger its share.

What are Boost Points and Boost Bonuses?
Boost Points are what you spend to boost projects. Everyone gets some for free, holding ART tops

you up each week, and the more you contribute the more you get. Unspent Boost Points never
expire: they carry over from drive to drive and season to season, and you only lose them when you
spend them. Boost Bonuses are how boosts pay out each Fund Drive: every drive has a bonus pot,
and the boosts a project or Fund receives win it a share, on a deliberately gentle curve so bonus
money spreads wide. The pot starts at $50,000 and grows as the whole community crosses
collective fundraising milestones. Rank and cash prizes are set separately, by money raised (sales
plus match unlocked). We are testing this split as a one-week experiment starting with the
Harvest Fund Drive; if it makes the game worse, we roll back to the old way and say so.

How do I cast Boosts? What is WIN?
Tap the Boost button on any Fund Drive card to cast Boost Points, one tap at a time. Want to go
straight for the top? WIN is the final tile in the suggested amounts above the sponsor and buy field
on the Fund Drive card, on both project and Fund pages. Clicking it automatically computes the
most cost-effective combination of your Boost Points and money to put that project or Fund into
first place on the leaderboard. The Boost button itself has also been tuned for speed, so casting is
smoother and more reliable than ever.

 Quests
What are Quests?
Quests are time-limited mini-games in the grow app that reward creators for actions that grow the
ecosystem. Event Quests reward attending and participating in live Artizen events. Karma Quests
reward lifting up other creators’ projects. Sprint Quests are time-boxed leaderboard races with
ranked prizes. Raffle Quests mint numbered keys from qualifying actions (like Artifact sales) and
draw a winner live, provably fair, with the whole pot paid to the winner’s project. Outreach
Quests reward personally bringing your people into Artizen. Network Quests reward building
your network of contacts in the grow app. Match Quests match qualifying fresh Artifact sales
with extra Artifact purchases on the same project while the quest pool lasts. Each quest has its
own name, rules, and countdown, shown in the grow app while it runs.

Do quests pay dollars or Boost Points?
Both, depending on the quest. Karma, Sprint, and Raffle prizes usually land as Artifact purchases
on your project. Event, Outreach, and Network quests pay Boost Points, which you earn in the
grow app and spend on artizen.fund, boosting the projects and Funds you want to see win.
Unspent Boost Points never expire, and each quest card shows its live rates and caps.

What is Fund a Friend?
The first Karma mini-game. While the quest is live, buy Artifacts from another creator’s project in
the current Fund Drive and Venus buys the same dollar amount of Artifacts from your project,
dollar for dollar, usually within minutes. You need an active project in the drive to receive the
mirrored purchase, and each creator can receive up to $10,000 in mirrored purchases per quest.
See Fund a Friend for the full mechanics.

Do Venus’s mirrored purchases unlock match funding?
Yes. Mirrored purchases count like any other Artifact purchase: they add to your sales, unlock your
available match funding at that week’s Match Multiple, and lift your project up the leaderboard.

I backed a friend during a Karma quest but haven’t seen a mirror yet. What gives?
Mirrors usually land within minutes, but a few things can delay or stop one: the purchase
happened outside the quest window, you don’t have an active project in the current Fund Drive, or
you’ve reached that quest’s mirror cap. Open the grow app and refresh the quest card to see the
live status of your mirrors.

Who can play Quests?
Quests live in the grow app, which is for creators, fund directors, and curators. Most quests so far
are for creators with an active project, since rewards land as Artifact purchases on your project.
Each quest lists its own requirements in the grow app while it runs.

How do quest prizes pay out?
As real sales. When a quest closes, Venus buys the winners’ Artifacts for the prize amounts, and
during Karma quests she mirrors your generosity onto your project while the quest runs. Those
purchases count like any other Artifact sale: money raised, match unlocked, a climb up the
leaderboard.

What is the Fair Finish?
Sprints don’t end at the buzzer. At the scheduled close, a fresh 5-minute timer starts for the
current leader. If that leader holds first place for the full 5 minutes, winners are final. The timer
restarts only when a different project takes first place; sales that do not flip the leader do not
extend the race.

 Code of Conduct
Artizen works because people trust each other. Creators share unfinished work, curators make
judgment calls, and sponsors put real money behind strangers’ dreams. None of that survives
without basic decency. So here’s the deal:

Treat people with respect. Challenge ideas, not the person holding them. Disagree with
decisions all you want; never attack the person making them.

Feedback exists to make the work better. Give notes to help, not to wound. Critique the
work, never the maker.

No harassment, abuse, or intimidation. No personal attacks, no slurs, no threats, no targeting
someone because they declined your project or gave you feedback you didn’t like. Rejection is
part of every game; how you handle it is part of your reputation.

This applies everywhere Artizen gathers. The platform, our community chat in the Grow app,
live events and video calls, email, and DMs between members about Artizen business. If it
happens in our spaces or to our people, it counts.

Fund Directors and curators are held to a higher bar. Creators depend on you, and your words
carry the weight of your position. If you review someone’s work, you owe them a decent
decline. If someone declines yours, you owe them the same grace you’d want from your own
applicants.

One identity, one account. Don’t use fake accounts or alternate personas to continue a
conflict, evade a removal, or pile onto someone.
If someone makes you feel unsafe, tell us: support@artizen.fund. We take every report seriously
and we keep reporters’ identities confidential.

 House Vibes
The community chat in Grow is our shared living room. The Code of Conduct above covers safety;
these house rules cover the vibe:

Lift each other up. Celebrate wins, yours and everyone else’s. A launch, a sale, a first curation:
cheer it.

The chat runs on yes-and. Build on ideas instead of poking holes in them. Skepticism is useful
elsewhere; this room is for momentum.

Assume good intent. Text strips tone. When a message lands badly, give it the generous read
first; someone here almost always meant well.

Speak from “I”. Own your feelings, opinions, and experiences. Say “I think” and “I felt”, not
“everyone agrees”.

Praise in public, critique in private. Celebrate people where everyone can see it. When you
have a hard note for someone, bring it to them directly with care; lead with curiosity and aim to
make the work better.

All beliefs are welcome here, which is why politics stays out. Keep the room about creative
work and the people making it.

One thoughtful post beats five fast ones. Take a beat and write the good version.
Questions go to Venus in Help. Payouts, logistics, and how-does-this-work questions get real
answers 1:1 in the Help tab, so the room stays a room.

You joined a village, not an audience. Welcome newcomers, make introductions, and connect
people who should know each other.
Venus tends the chat. Posts that fit better in Help get moved there privately, and the question
actually gets answered.

 Sharing Links
Share your links! This room exists for exactly that: your projects, your fund, events you are hosting,
essays you wrote, work in progress, and the work of people you admire. If you made something or

Say what it is. A sentence of context turns a link into a conversation. What are you making?

replies, and boosts. Bare links mostly get scrolled past.

Artizen links are always welcome. Anything on artizen.fund, the newsletter, or an Artizen
event page can be dropped anytime, with or without context. And asking the room to heart,
boost, or repost official Artizen stuff is totally fine.

Celebrate other people. Sharing someone else’s project with their link is one of the best things
you can post here.

Share your own work as often as you like. Just bring something new each time: an update, a
milestone, a question. Reposting the same bare link over and over gets a gentle nudge from me
instead of a spot in the room.

to be something they are not, gambling or adult content, token shilling, and get-rich-quick

one of your links by mistake, tell me in Help; a human reviews every hold.
That is the whole policy. Post generously.

 Appeals & Returning
If you’re removed from Artizen and believe we got it wrong, you can appeal. Here’s how it works:

One written appeal, within 14 days. Write to support@artizen.fund and tell us what
happened, what you dispute, and what you would do differently. Character references are
welcome inside your letter, and there’s no quota. Campaigning for support in community
spaces ends the appeal.

Fresh eyes review it. Your appeal is reviewed by someone who didn’t make the original
removal decision. Reporters stay confidential and are never made to face the person they
reported.

The removal stays in effect while we review. You’ll get an answer within 14 days. One appeal
per removal, and the decision is final.

The path back is time and demonstrated change. After 12 months, anyone removed may write
to the same address and ask to return. Show us changed behavior and we’ll take a genuine look.
