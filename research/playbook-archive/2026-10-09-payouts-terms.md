 Code of Conduct

Artizen works because people trust each other. Creators share unfinished work, curators make judgment calls, and sponsors put real money behind strangers’ dreams. None of that survives without basic decency. So here’s the deal:
Treat people with respect. Challenge ideas, not the person holding them. Disagree with decisions all you want; never attack the person making them.

Feedback exists to make the work better. Give notes to help, not to wound. Critique the work, never the maker.

No harassment, abuse, or intimidation. No personal attacks, no slurs, no threats, no targeting someone because they declined your project or gave you feedback you didn’t like. Rejection is part of every game; how you handle it is part of your reputation.

This applies everywhere Artizen gathers. The platform, our community chat in the Grow app, live events and video calls, email, and DMs between members about Artizen business. If it happens in our spaces or to our people, it counts.

Fund Directors and curators are held to a higher bar. Creators depend on you, and your words carry the weight of your position. If you review someone’s work, you owe them a decent decline. If someone declines yours, you owe them the same grace you’d want from your own applicants.

One identity, one account. Don’t use fake accounts or alternate personas to continue a conflict, evade a removal, or pile onto someone.

If someone makes you feel unsafe, tell us: support@artizen.fund. We take every report seriously and we keep reporters’ identities confidential.

 House Vibes

The community chat in Grow is our shared living room. The Code of Conduct above covers safety; these house rules cover the vibe:
Lift each other up. Celebrate wins, yours and everyone else’s. A launch, a sale, a first curation: cheer it.

The chat runs on yes-and. Build on ideas instead of poking holes in them. Skepticism is useful elsewhere; this room is for momentum.

Assume good intent. Text strips tone. When a message lands badly, give it the generous read first; someone here almost always meant well.

Speak from “I”. Own your feelings, opinions, and experiences. Say “I think” and “I felt”, not “everyone agrees”.

Praise in public, critique in private. Celebrate people where everyone can see it. When you have a hard note for someone, bring it to them directly with care; lead with curiosity and aim to make the work better.

All beliefs are welcome here, which is why politics stays out. Keep the room about creative work and the people making it.

One thoughtful post beats five fast ones. Take a beat and write the good version.

Questions go to Venus in Help. Payouts, logistics, and how-does-this-work questions get real answers 1:1 in the Help tab, so the room stays a room.

You joined a village, not an audience. Welcome newcomers, make introductions, and connect people who should know each other.

Venus tends the chat. Posts that fit better in Help get moved there privately, and the question actually gets answered.

 Sharing Links

Share your links! This room exists for exactly that: your projects, your fund, events you are hosting, essays you wrote, work in progress, and the work of people you admire. If you made something or you love something, we want to see it.
A few things make links land well here:
Say what it is. A sentence of context turns a link into a conversation. What are you making? What happened this week? Why does it matter to you? Posts with a little story get hearts, replies, and boosts. Bare links mostly get scrolled past.

Artizen links are always welcome. Anything on artizen.fund, the newsletter, or an Artizen event page can be dropped anytime, with or without context. And asking the room to heart, boost, or repost official Artizen stuff is totally fine.

Celebrate other people. Sharing someone else’s project with their link is one of the best things you can post here.

Share your own work as often as you like. Just bring something new each time: an update, a milestone, a question. Reposting the same bare link over and over gets a gentle nudge from me instead of a spot in the room.

A few things do not fly: link shorteners that hide where a link leads, lookalike domains pretending to be something they are not, gambling or adult content, token shilling, and get-rich-quick anything. Those get held before the room sees them, and genuine scams are removed. If I ever hold one of your links by mistake, tell me in Help; a human reviews every hold.
That is the whole policy. Post generously.

 Projects

Early Exit: There are two ways a project can Early Exit. In both cases the project is archived and the creator is paid their Artifact sales only.  Payouts

Artizen processes payments for Artifact and sponsorship purchases and issues creator payouts after each season.

Funds collected from Artifact sales and sponsorships are held by Artizen during the season and are not available for withdrawal until payouts are calculated and issued.

We process payments, deduct fees, and distribute funds to creators after each season.

Payouts may take several weeks to process due to audits and regulations.

All Artizen fees are collected upfront at the point of sale. At payout, only actual transfer costs (if any) are deducted. These fees are routed to the Endowment, where they help fund future match awards and prizes for creators.

Creators and Fund Directors are independent contractors and are solely responsible for their own taxes, reporting, and compliance obligations.

Artifact sales, match funding, and weekly Fund Drive cash prizes are paid in U.S. dollars to a bank account or in USDC to a crypto wallet, at the creator's choice. Only the season finale prize is paid in ART.

To receive payouts, creators must complete identity, tax, and compliance checks as required by law.

We cannot issue payouts to sanctioned individuals, entities, or jurisdictions.

Artizen reserves the right to delay or withhold payouts if discrepancies, compliance issues, or financial rule violations (like wash sales or fraud) are identified.

Artizen may convert digital assets into fiat or other digital assets for operational purposes.

Payments & Payout Terms: Artizen processes payments for Artifact purchases and Fund sponsorships and is the merchant of record for those transactions.  Enforcement & Removal

Most problems get solved with a conversation. But when they don’t, here’s how it works:
When we get a report, we review it. We look at the evidence, talk to the people involved when we can, and keep the reporter’s identity confidential.

The response fits the behavior. Depending on what happened, that can mean a conversation, a formal warning, removal from specific spaces, or removal from Artizen entirely. A clear warning means the next incident has consequences.

Safety comes first. If someone’s conduct makes others feel unsafe, we can remove them from Artizen at any time, even on a first incident. Nobody should have to share a community with someone who mistreats them.

You always get paid what you’ve earned. Removal does not cancel Artifact sales that are due. On a normal season close, amounts due under the normal payout rules are still paid. On Early Exit, only Artifact sales are due.

Funds wind down in an orderly way. If a removed member directs a Fund, we close it carefully: curated projects are told what it means for them, sponsors are made whole or their support is redirected, and unused Endowment match reverts to the Endowment.

Rule-breaking still has teeth. Fraud, wash trading, boost manipulation, and fake accounts may cost you your account, your projects, and payouts tied to the cheating.

Our decisions are final. Enforcement calls are made at our sole discretion. If you have questions about a decision that affects you, write to support@artizen.fund and a human will answer. If you’ve been removed, you can appeal once; see Appeals & Returning below.

If you see something wrong, report it to support@artizen.fund.

 Appeals & Returning

If you’re removed from Artizen and believe we got it wrong, you can appeal. Here’s how it works:
One written appeal, within 14 days. Write to support@artizen.fund and tell us what happened, what you dispute, and what you would do differently. Character references are welcome inside your letter, and there’s no quota. Campaigning for support in community spaces ends the appeal.

Fresh eyes review it. Your appeal is reviewed by someone who didn’t make the original removal decision. Reporters stay confidential and are never made to face the person they reported.

The removal stays in effect while we review. You’ll get an answer within 14 days. One appeal per removal, and the decision is final.

The path back is time and demonstrated change. After 12 months, anyone removed may write to the same address and ask to return. Show us changed behavior and we’ll take a genuine look.

 Platform Continuity

If Artizen cannot keep running a season under the normal rules, we will close projects out cleanly rather than leave payouts hanging.
Artizen may elect Early Exit for any or all projects if:
Artizen pauses, winds down, or closes the platform;

it becomes impractical to complete normal season payouts for operational, legal, financial, or regulatory reasons; or

Artizen otherwise determines that continuing under the normal payout rules is not viable.

What creators are owed. On an Early Exit, only Artifact sales are due. Match funding, prizes, bonuses, and other awards are not due. Sales already collected for a project are still paid under the normal payout process, minus any amounts Artizen cannot pay because of chargebacks, uncleared payments, sanctions, or fraud. Artizen may pay those sales in a single close-out batch or in installments rather than waiting for a full season audit.
Notice. Artizen will notify affected creators using the email on the account and, where practical, an in-product notice. A platform-wide Early Exit may be announced once for everyone.
Funds. If Artizen elects Early Exit across some or all of the platform, affected Funds wind down. Unused match, unused Endowment earmarks, and unpaid Fund prizes revert to the Endowment. They do not roll to the next drive or the next season. Sponsorships remain voluntary and non-refundable except where required by law.
Season and leaderboards. An platform-wide Early Exit may end a Fund Drive or a season early. Open leaderboards, pending prizes, and pending Boost Bonuses do not vest. Rankings at the moment of notice are not a promise to pay.
What this does not change. Immutable Endowment mechanics coded into smart contracts still control ART issuance, floor, and redemption. This section is about project payouts and platform operations, not rewriting on-chain rules.

Full satisfaction and release. You and Artizen may disagree about whether any match, prize, or bonus was owed. Submitting a payout claim is an offer to resolve that dispute by accepting the Sales Amount as the only payment Artizen will make for the listed projects and seasons. When you have received the full Sales Amount, Artizen’s payment obligations to you for those projects and seasons are fully satisfied, and you release Artizen Works Inc. from claims for those projects and seasons. This is a settlement. Neither side admits liability.
Sole discretion. Whether to elect Artizen Early Exit, for which projects, and when, is Artizen’s call. The money rule does not change with the reason: sales are due; match, prizes, and boosts are not.

 Disputes

Start with us: support@artizen.fund.

By using Artizen you agree to these rules. You accept that Artizen is experimental, carries risk, and may result in the loss of all funds.

Play fair. Be honest. Respect property. That’s how markets work, and that’s how Artizen works.
