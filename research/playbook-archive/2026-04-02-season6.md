##  Quickstart

Artizen is a funding platform for breakthroughs in art, science, technology, and culture.

Each season:

1. People launch new funds to support their community.
2. Mission-aligned brands and individuals sponsor funds.
3. Creators submit projects to any Funds they qualify for.
4. Everyone votes - votes act as a signal, not a gate. Fund admins make the final call on approvals.
5. Projects sell Artifacts - a new kind of digital collectible - to their fans.
6. Each sale unlocks match funding from approved Funds - and during Boosts, that funding multiplies.
7. Sell the most Artifacts, win cash prizes at season’s end for our Endowment.

##  Gameplay

Whether you're a creator, community leader, or sponsor - here’s how to play the game of Artizen:

###  Setup

**Launch a Fund**
Support the projects that matter most to your community by launching your own Fund.

**Become a Sponsor**
Raise money for your community and earn rewards by sponsoring a Fund.

**Submit Your Projects**
Win cash prizes and unlock match funding by selling cultural Artifacts to your fans.

###  Curation

**Earn Voting Power**
Back projects, sponsor Funds, or engage to earn voting power.

**Vote for Projects**
Cast votes for your favorite projects. Votes are a signal - not a gate.

**Get Approved**
Fund admins review submissions and approve any project that meets their eligibility criteria.

###  Competition

**Sell Artifacts**
Fans buy cultural Artifacts to earn rewards and support projects they love.

**Unlock Match Funding**
Once approved by a Fund, every $1 you raise from fans unlocks $1 from that Fund - up to a cap.

**Win Cash Prizes**
At the end of each season, top projects also win cash prizes from the Artizen Endowment.

**Power Up with Boosts**
Throughout the season, time-limited Boosts multiply your match funding or award bonus cash prizes. When a Boost goes live, that's your moment to rally.

###  Example

Josh launches the Public Art Fund to support creators in Portland. He rallies local businesses and neighbors and raises $10,000 in sponsor funding, which also unlocks $10,000 in cash prizes from the Artizen Endowment. So there’s $20,000 in play.

Three projects apply and get approved:

-  Street Mural - Victor’s community painting project
-  Tree Sculpture - Sarah’s whimsical public statue
-  Little Library - Zach’s neighborhood book exchange

Because they’re approved, each project can unlock up to 30% of the Fund’s match pool. At the start, the Fund holds $10,000. That gives each project a maximum match of $3,000.

But here’s the twist: It’s first come, first served.

#### Victor Moves Fast

As soon as Victor’s project Street Mural is approved, he gets to work. He emails everyone he knows. Posts on social. Calls friends and supporters. Hustles hard.

In the first week, Victor sells $3,000 worth of Artifacts. That instantly unlocks $3,000 in match funding - his full cap.

Victor’s numbers now look like this:

- $3,000 from fans
- $3,000 matched by the Fund
- **$6,000 total**

After Victor claims his match, the Fund balance drops to $7,000.

#### Sarah Catches a Boost

Sarah sees what Victor just pulled off and doesn’t waste time. But the game has shifted. Her cap is no longer based on the original $10,000. It’s based on what’s left. Thirty percent of the remaining $7,000 gives her a Fund match cap of $2,100. But there's a 3× Match Boost running - funded by the Artizen Endowment - with a per-project cap of $10,000. That means for every dollar her fans spend, the Endowment adds three dollars on top of her normal Fund match.

She rallies her community and raises $2,500 from fans. That unlocks $2,100 in match funding from the Fund (her cap) plus $7,500 from the Endowment (3× her fan sales from the Boost). Two separate funding sources, stacking together.

Now Sarah’s project Tree Sculpture stands at:

- $2,500 from fans
- $2,100 matched from Fund
- $7,500 Boost funding from Endowment (3× fan sales)
- **$12,100 total**

The Fund balance drops to $4,900 - because the Boost money came from the Endowment, not the Fund. The pool is still healthy for the next project.

#### Zach Starts Late

Zach waits a bit too long to push Little Library. By the time he gets in the game, the Boost has ended. His Fund match cap is 30% of $4,900, which comes out to $1,470.

Zach raises $3,000 from supporters - impressive! But without the Boost, his only match comes from the Fund: $1,470.

Zach’s totals:

- $3,000 from fans
- $1,470 matched from Fund
- **$4,470 total**

Same rules. Same percentage. But Zach missed the Boost window. Sarah's timing - rallying her community during the 3× Boost - unlocked $7,500 in extra Endowment funding that Zach can't access now. Timing is everything.

#### End of Season Prizes

Now the $10,000 prize pool kicks in. Projects are ranked by how much they raised:

| Project | Raised | Prize |
|---|---|---|
|  Tree Sculpture (Sarah) | $12,100 | $5,000 |
|  Street Mural (Victor) | $6,000 | $3,000 |
|  Little Library (Zach) | $4,470 | $2,000 |

Sarah's Boost timing vaulted her way past Victor in total dollars raised. Meanwhile Zach's community showed up strong - but missing the Boost window cost him thousands in Endowment funding.

And remember - Victor, Sarah, and Zach aren’t limited to just one Fund. They could also get approved by other Funds, unlock more match funding, and win even more prize money.

Same sales.
Stacked rewards.

 **The takeaway?** Get approved by multiple Funds. Sell as many Artifacts as you can as soon as you can. That’s how you unlock the most match funding and cash prizes. That’s how you win big.

##  Projects

We fund bold ideas in art, science, technology, and culture - at any stage, in any medium. From oil paintings to open-source code, from raw sparks to finished works.

What we look for:

- **Originality.** Is it fresh? Surprising? Unlike anything we’ve seen?
- **Impact.** Will it change lives, revitalize communities, or regenerate ecosystems?
- **Feasibility.** Can you pull it off with the team, time, and money you’ve got?

Anyone can submit - for free. But if you want to unlock match funding and win cash prizes, you’ll need to rise above the rest. Here’s how.

###  Create a Profile

To get started, create a project profile. This is the public face of your project - the first thing seen by your fans, friends, and supporters. So make it dope.

**REQUIRED**

- **Project Title** - Short and specific (max 75 characters)
- **Logline** - One-sentence hook that captures your big idea (max 140 characters)
- **Tags** - Describe your project's focus and field
- **What are you making?** - Say it plainly, like you’re talking to a friend (250 characters)
- **How will it impact the world?** - Tell us why it matters (250 characters)
- **What progress have you made?** - Show momentum (250 characters)
- **Why are you the right person?** - Why are you uniquely suited? (250 characters)
- **Artifact** - An image, gif, or video that captures the soul of your project. See guidelines

**OPTIONAL (but powerful)**

- **Featured Video** - Link to a YouTube or Vimeo video.
- **Rewards** - Power-ups and perks for fans who buy your Artifacts. See guidelines
- **Pitch Deck** - Upload a PDF that goes deeper into your story. See guidelines

###  Get Funded

Once your profile’s ready, it's time to start fundraising.

**SUBMIT TO FUNDS **
Research active Funds and submit to every Fund you qualify for. Read the descriptions. Study the eligibility criteria. Then tailor your submission - explain why you're a good fit. The more Funds you submit to, the more shots you have at getting approved.

**GET APPROVED **
Fund admins review every submission and decide whether your project fits their eligibility criteria. Votes play a big role here - they signal how deep and broad your community support is and help ensure your project gets full consideration. But votes don’t auto-approve you. Final decisions always rest with Fund Directors and admins, who approve or remove projects based on fit with their mission.

**RAISE MONEY **
Once you’re approved, you’re in the game. Start selling Artifacts to your fans. Every $1 you raise unlocks $1 from each Fund backing your project - up to your cap. Each project can unlock a maximum of 30% of a Fund’s balance, and that cap shrinks as other projects claim match funding. Move fast or miss out. The more you sell, the more you earn - and top sellers also win extra cash prizes from the Artizen Endowment.

###  Boosts

Throughout the season, Artizen runs Boosts - time-limited events that supercharge your fundraising. Think of them as power-ups. They come in two types:

** Sales Sprints**

A short competition (usually 2-5 days) with a cash prize pool funded by the Artizen Endowment. The projects that sell the most Artifacts during the sprint win prizes - on top of everything else you earn. Sprint prizes are bonus money for moving fast, paid directly from the Endowment.

** Match Boosts**

A Match Boost adds bonus funding from the Artizen Endowment on top of your normal Fund match. Each Boost has two variables: a multiplier (e.g. 3×) and a per-project cap. During a 3× Boost, every dollar your fans spend on Artifacts unlocks three extra dollars from the Endowment - on top of whatever match funding you earn from your Fund. The cap limits how much Endowment money any single project can receive. Once you hit it, the Boost bonus stops but your regular Fund match rate continues.

** Strategy**

- **Watch the Boosts page.** When a Boost goes live, rally your community immediately.
- **Stack your advantages.** During a Sprint with an active Match Boost, every sale earns fan revenue + boosted match funding + sprint ranking. This is when the biggest gains happen.
- **Fair finish.** If a project overtakes the leader right at a Sprint deadline, the sprint extends automatically to give the displaced leader a chance to respond.

###  Payouts

How and when you get paid.

Each Artifact costs $10 (plus applicable fees at checkout). Artifact sales count toward your season payout. After the season ends, we calculate final totals (sales + match awards + prizes), complete reviews, and then issue payouts.

Payouts are issued after the season ends so we can finalize results, confirm cleared transactions, and process payouts accurately.

Every $1 in sales also unlocks $1 from each Fund your project is curated for, while money lasts. Match awards are tracked throughout the season and included in your final payout after the season ends. Prize money works differently. It’s conditional. Only top projects win it, based on total Artifact sales.

At the end of the season, you’ll receive one combined payout:

- Artifact Sales +
- Match Funding +
- Any Cash Prizes

After a season ends, payouts are processed in batches following a review and audit period. Most creators are paid within a few weeks, though some payouts may take longer depending on size, review requirements, and payment clearing timelines. Artizen collects all fees upfront when Artifacts are purchased or Funds are sponsored, so there are no additional deductions at payout beyond any actual transfer costs.

Artifact sales and match awards are paid in US dollars to a bank account or in USDC to your Artizen wallet. Prize awards are paid in ART, a digital token used in the Artizen ecosystem.

##  Funds

Launch a Fund. Grow your community.

A Fund on Artizen is simply a pool of capital to support creators. Anyone can launch a Fund - and we make it easy to raise money and curate the best projects.

Here’s the magic: every $1 raised for your Fund equals $3 of impact - $1 from sponsors, $1 from fans, and $1+ from our Endowment. The money is pooled and awarded to top projects at the end of each season.

It’s the best way to amplify your impact for the creators you love.

**Important legal note:** Funds on Artizen are not investment vehicles or securities. Sponsorships are not investments and do not provide equity, profit rights, or financial returns. Artizen has consulted outside legal counsel who has reviewed this structure and confirmed that Funds are community funding programs, not regulated investment products.

###  Apply to Launch

We look for passionate leaders rooted in creative communities - from basket weaving to biohacking. If approved, we kickstart your Fund with at least a $1,000 contribution.

Your Fund must include:

- **Fund Title** - Short, clear, and focused.
- **Logline** - One sentence that captures your mission and who you support.
- **Tags** - Keywords that define your focus. (e.g., “Afrofuturism,” “Climate Tech,” “Agentic AI”)
- **Description** - A short paragraph that explains what your community stands for. What’s your mission? Why does it matter?
- **Eligibility Requirements** - Who qualifies for support? Be specific. (e.g., “Projects led by women building open-source tech”)
- **Hero Image** - One strong 16:9 image that sets the vibe. No text or graphic overlays.

###  Curate Projects

Curate the best projects with your community.

**Define Your Focus**
Start by defining clear eligibility requirements. What kind of creators or projects do you want to support? Be specific and intentional.

**Let Your Community Vote**
All projects receive votes from fans and the community. Votes are a powerful signal - they show depth and breadth of support and help you prioritize which projects deserve your full attention..

**Approve Best Submissions**
Approve projects that meet your criteria. If not, remove them with a short note - creators can revise and resubmit. This isn’t about judging quality, just eligibility.

It’s a simple system. You ensure every project fits your mission.

###  Get Sponsors

Anyone can sponsor your Fund - from individual supporters to major brands and foundations.

Sponsors get:

- **Votes:** 10 votes per $1 contributed.
- **Impact:** Every $1 they give is matched by $1 from fans and at least $1 from our Endowment.
- **Rewards:** You can offer shoutouts, visibility, or perks at higher tiers

When designing sponsor tiers, keep it simple. 3 to 5 tiers is plenty. Offer rewards that align with your mission. Name tiers creatively (“Signal Booster,” “Visionary,” etc). And use clean visuals without text or graphic overlays overlays.

###  Award Funding

Here’s how money flows through your Fund - and into the hands of creators:

**Sponsor Money is Pooled**
Every dollar raised from brands, foundations, and individuals goes straight into a match funding pool. Artizen takes no cut. Approved projects don’t split this evenly - instead, each project can unlock unlock up to 30% of your Fund’s balance. As projects claim match funding, the remaining pool shrinks - so later projects have lower caps.

*Example:* Your Fund raises $10,000. Each approved project can unlock up to $3,000 (30%). If one project moves fast and unlocks their full $3,000, only $7,000 remains. Now the next project’s cap drops to $2,100. And so on. Same rules. Different outcomes.

**Creators Unlock Match Funding**
Approved projects compete by selling Artifacts. Every $1 raised unlocks $1 from your Fund - up to their current cap. Once they hit it, they can’t unlock more from your Fund. Unused money stays in the pool for other projects.

*Example:* If a project raises $1,500 from fans, it unlocks $1,500 from your Fund - and the remaining $500 rolls over to the next season.

**Prize Money is Awarded**
For every $1 raised from sponsors, Artizen unlocks $1 in prize money from the Endowment. At season’s end, that prize pool is fully distributed to projects based on their ranking on your Fund leaderboard.

We use a halving-style curve - top projects earn more, but every ranked project gets paid. The exact math may evolve, but the principle stays the same: Reward excellence. Pay everyone.

###  Earn Benefits

Fund Directors are compensated for operating their Fund and providing services including: community building, marketing, partnerships, curation, and program management.

Compensation is structured as follows:

- 20% of sponsor contributions to their Fund.
- 10% of prize money unlocked from the Artizen Endowment.

This compensation reflects services rendered and is not tied to investment performance.

Fund Directors are not required to fundraise and may choose how actively they promote their Fund.

All compensation is clearly disclosed to sponsors in advance.

##  Artifacts

A new kind of digital collectible.

Artifacts capture the essence of a project and its intended impact on the world. They can be a sketch, concept art, looping gif, photo, animation, video - any media that marks a milestone and channels the vibe of a project.

To be clear, Artifacts are not projects themselves. Rather, they are unique works of art that represent the "big ideas" behind a project.

###  Collecting Artifacts

Artifacts cost $10 and are sold as open edition NFTs throughout the season. You can buy as many as you want from any project, and your purchase supports the project and increases what the creator can earn this season. Each sale also unlocks additional match funding, boosts, and prizes - amplifying your impact.

For every Artifact purchased, we mint multiple copies:

- One for the Fan who bought it
- One for the Creator
- One for Artizen

This way, everyone has skin in the game - and a share in the upside. At the end of the season, sales close, and the total number of Artifacts minted becomes the fixed supply.

###  Investing in Impact

Artifacts aren’t equity. They don’t give you shares, control, or IP rights in a project. With Artifacts, you don’t own the project - you own the impact.

Like all works of art, the value of an Artifact comes from its aesthetics, rarity, and emotional resonance. But its true worth depends on the impact of the project it represents. The greater the impact, the more valuable the Artifact.

For example, imagine a research project in material science. Their Artifact might simply be a math equation scribbled in a notebook. But if that equation changes how we produces energy, its value could skyrocket.

Most Artifacts will go to zero - because most projects fail. But a few will transform the world. And those will be priceless.

###  Artifact Guidelines

Your Artifact is the main visual for your project. Make it beautiful. Artifacts are NOT trailers, ads, posters, screenshots, pitch decks, or marketing collateral. As such, we do NOT allow any text, logos, or graphic overlays - unless they’re integral to the art itself (e.g., graphic design or collage).

** DO**

- Use a SQUARE format (1:1)
- Stand alone as a unique work of art
- Document an important milestone
- Capture the aesthetic and vibe of your project

** DON’T**

- NO TEXT
- NO GRAPHIC OVERLAYS
- NO LOGOS

###  Technical Specs

- **IMAGE:** Square, 1000px or greater, .jpg or .png file type, 10MB or smaller, and NO text or graphic overlays.
- **GIF (optional):** Square, 500px or greater, .gif file type, 50MB or smaller, and NO text or graphic overlays.
- **VIDEO (optional):** Square, 45 seconds or less, no more than 100MB, .mp4, loopable, and NO text or graphic overlays.

##  Endowment

The Artizen Endowment is the engine that helps fund prizes, match boosts, and long-term support for breakthrough work. It grows through a mix of platform revenue, sponsorship activity, and on-chain participation.

With immutable rules like Bitcoin, ART is designed to fund creativity over the long arc. The system tracks reserves - including cash, crypto, and cultural Artifacts - and includes on-chain mechanics that can support redemptions under the rules of the smart contracts. Like all crypto systems, this is experimental and carries risk.

###  How It Works

The Endowment is powered by a Revnet - an autonomous financial engine built on Juicebox. For the full technical breakdown of how ART tokens work - minting, splits, buybacks, and redemptions - see  How It Works.

###  The Prize Pool

The Prize Pool is what turns ART into fuel for creators.

- Denominated in ART and held in a SAFE during each season
- Every $1 in sponsorship or Artifact sales unlocks $1 in prize money from the Endowment
- Only unlocked ART is awarded; unused balances roll forward into the next season
- At season’s end, unlocked ART is distributed to top projects on Fund and Global leaderboards

This design ties Endowment payouts directly to community activity - ensuring the pool grows with demand, rather than shrinking from overuse.

###  What Backs ART

The Endowment tracks reserves and assets held across approved accounts and on-chain contracts, including:

- USD + USDC reserves (insured deposits + stablecoins)
- Crypto assets (BTC, ETH, SOL, and ART itself)
- Cultural Artifacts (a copy of every Artifact sold)
- The Prize Pool (ART unlocked by sponsors and sales)
- Match Funds contributed by sponsors

These are the components we use to track the Endowment’s activity and reserves over time. Asset values can fluctuate and may be illiquid.

###  Issuance Schedule

ART follows a fixed issuance schedule like Bitcoin, designed to reward early participants and create long-term scarcity. Each stage reduces token issuance and doubles the mint price on a predictable timeline.

**Stage 1 - Genesis**

- Starts Sept 17, 2025
- 3 cycles × 35 days
- Starts at 10,000 ART / USD
- Issuance halves each cycle (price doubles)

**Stage 2 - Growth**

- Starts Dec 31, 2025
- 7 cycles × 120 days
- Starts at 1,250 ART / USD
- Issuance halves each cycle

**Stage 3 - Scale**

- Starts Apr 19, 2028
- Annual cycles (365 days)
- Starting rate ~9.7 ART / USD (rounded down)
- Price doubles each year
- At high mint prices, issuance slows - eventually becoming deflationary

###  Why This Model Works

- **Immutable rules.** Growth is hardwired into code.
- **Rising floor + exponential ceiling.** Predictable guardrails balance risk and reward.
- **Patience pays.** Short-term seller fees punish flippers, reward holders.
- **Built-in demand.** Even if speculators leave, Artizen revenue buys ART every season.
- **Elastic supply.** Minted with demand, burned when redeemed. Over time, supply growth slows and can reverse.
- **Prize Pool multiplier.** Every $1 from sponsors and fans is doubled by the Endowment.
- **Cultural collateral.** Every Artifact sold adds to the Treasury, indexing creativity itself.

##  FAQ

Learning the rules to any new game can be confusing. We get it. Artizen mixes elements of crowdfunding, crypto, and community competition - so of course there are a lot of moving parts. No question is dumb. If you’re unsure about something, you’re not alone. This FAQ clears up the most common points of confusion so you can focus on playing - and winning - the game.

###  Artifacts

**What exactly is an Artifact?**

An Artifact is a digital collectible that represents a project. Each sale supports the creator, unlocks match funding and prizes, and becomes part of a fixed edition at season’s end.

**If I buy an Artifact, do I own the project?**

No. Artifacts don’t give you control, equity, or rights. You own the collectible itself. Its value comes from the project’s cultural or social impact - not financial claims.

**Why would I buy more than one Artifact?**

More Artifacts mean more votes, more rewards, and more direct support for the creator. Plus, if the project succeeds, your Artifacts could appreciate in value and become culturally important collectibles. Think of Andy Warhol’s silkscreen prints - at the time they were just multiples, but as his work reshaped the art world, those prints skyrocketed in value. If a project on Artizen changes the world of art, science, technology, or culture, you’ll wish you held more than one.

**Why should fans spend $10 on an Artifact that might be worthless?**

Most Artifacts will go to zero. That’s honest. But a few projects will change the world. Those Artifacts will be priceless. Fans buy both for the upside and to support projects they believe in. The $10 isn’t charity - it’s skin in the game.

**Are Artifacts regulated as securities?**

No. Artifacts are unique works of art - open-edition NFTs under the hood. Art is the last great unregulated market. Anyone can buy and sell art. That’s why Artifacts are digital collectibles, not equity, share, stocks, or securities.

###  Projects

**How does match funding work?**

For every $1 in Artifact sales, $1 is unlocked from each Fund that has approved your project - up to your cap. Each project can unlock a maximum of 30% of a Fund’s balance, and that cap shrinks as other projects claim match funding. Sell early to unlock more. Get approved by more Funds to multiply the effect.

**How is prize money different from match funding?**

Match funding is tied directly to sales - you sell, you unlock it, subject to the Fund’s remaining balance and your cap. Prize money is different. It comes from the Endowment and is awarded based on rank at the end of the season. Projects compete on both Fund leaderboards and the global leaderboard, and prize pools are fully distributed using a halving-style curve. You do not need Fund approval to win prizes - only sales.

**How and when do creators actually get paid?**

At the end of each season. That way all sales, matches, and prizes can be audited and paid together. Payments usually arrive within a few days but can take up to 60 days for audits to be completed and for payments to be cleared.

Artifact sales and match funding are paid in USDC (a U.S. dollar stablecoin). Prize money is paid in ART, Artizen’s token. ART can be held, burned for USDC, or sold on the market.

**Can creators buy Artifacts from their own project?**

Yes. Creators are allowed to support their own projects. This is common when creators use personal funds or grants that can’t be routed directly through Artizen.

To keep the game fair, all sales, match funding, and prizes are held until the season ends - so creators cannot recycle funds or artificially amplify results mid-season.

**Who decides which projects get curated?**

Fund Directors and admins decide which projects are approved. They review every submission and approve projects that meet their eligibility criteria and fit the spirit of their Fund.

Votes matter - they signal how much real support a project has and help admins prioritize what to review - but votes do not auto-approve anyone. Final decisions always rest with Fund Directors and admins.

**What if a creator raises money but doesn’t deliver?**

That risk is real. Creators keep their sales, but must use awards to advance their project. We expect updates, and projects can be removed if they misrepresent themselves. Failure is part of discovery. Markets reward what works and punish what doesn’t - that’s how progress happens.

**What if a creator takes the money and disappears?**

It will happen. Risk is real. Our rules require creators to use awards to advance their projects, but rules alone can’t stop bad actors - scammers gonna scam. The best guard against this is reputation. Creators who don’t deliver will be booted from the platform, and their chances of raising again will vanish. Over time, the market weeds out bad faith players and rewards those who consistently build and deliver.

###  Payouts

**Why do payouts happen after the season ends?**

Payouts happen after the season ends to keep the game fair. If creators could receive funds during a season, they could recycle that money back into their own project to unlock more match funding or climb the leaderboard, creating an unfair feedback loop. Holding funds until the season ends prevents this kind of self-reinforcing behavior and ensures that all match funding and prizes are earned through real, external support.

Delaying payouts also allows us to finalize rankings, confirm Artifact sales and match funding across Funds, and ensure that all payments have fully cleared before money is distributed. Together, these safeguards protect creators, sponsors, and the integrity of the competition and makes traditional wash sales and self-reinforcing loops impossible during a season.

**How long will it take to receive my payout?**

Payouts begin shortly after each season ends and are processed in batches. Most creators receive their payout within a few weeks. In some cases - especially for larger payouts or where additional review is required - the process can take longer, but all payouts are completed within 60 days of the season ending.

Before releasing funds, we finalize season results, confirm Artifact sales and match funding, ensure leaderboard rankings and prize allocations are correct, and verify that all credit card, bank transfers, and other payments have fully cleared. We also complete required tax and compliance checks so payouts are issued correctly and lawfully.

Our goal is to pay creators as quickly as possible while maintaining a fair and trusted system for everyone. If we need anything from you to complete your payout, we’ll reach out directly. Otherwise, you’ll receive a confirmation as soon as your payout is on the way.

**Why do some creators get paid earlier than others?**

Payouts are processed in batches, and different payouts require different levels of review. Smaller payouts are usually faster to verify and can often be processed sooner. Larger payouts may require additional review, compliance checks, or clearing time, and in some cases may be handled separately. The goal is to pay everyone as quickly as possible while keeping the process accurate and compliant.

**What do I need to provide in order to get paid?**

Before we can release a payout, we need you to confirm who is legally receiving the funds and provide the appropriate tax documentation. We may also ask you to confirm basic compliance acknowledgments required to process payments safely and lawfully. If anything is missing or doesn’t match, we’ll follow up, and your payout may be delayed until it’s resolved.

We may be required to collect tax forms and issue tax reporting documents depending on your jurisdiction and the type of recipient.

**How are payouts issued, and what’s the difference between USDC and ART?**

Artifact sales and match funding are paid in USDC, a U.S. dollar stablecoin. End-of-season prize money is paid in ART, Artizen’s native token. ART represents a stake in the Artizen Endowment and the Artizen platform. Some creators choose to hold ART long-term if they believe Artizen will continue to grow, while others may choose to redeem or sell some portion for liquidity. What you do with ART is up to you.

**Why is prize money paid in ART?**

Prize money is paid in ART because it aligns creators with the long-term growth of the Artizen Endowment. Instead of prizes being a one-time cash distribution, ART gives winners a stake in the system that funds future seasons. This design rewards creators who contribute to Artizen’s momentum and gives them the option to participate in the upside as the platform grows.

Prizes follow a halving formula: the top project gets half the pool, the next gets half of what’s left, and so on. And we’re always tweaking the formula with one goal in mind: to reward the best projects while making sure every creator who ranks feels their effort was valued.

**How does Artizen make money?**

These fees are collected upfront at the point of purchase and flow directly into the Artizen Endowment, which awards match funding and prizes for creators. We also generate revenue through our Pro subscription, which unlocks premium voting and creator tools. And we earn 10% of every ART token purchase. And we earn 10% of every ART token purchase.

**Isn’t this just Kickstarter with extra steps?**

No. Kickstarter is a one-off tool to collect money from your own network - unless you go viral, that’s the limit. Artizen takes that same effort and amplifies it. For every $1 you raise from a fan, you can unlock $1 in match funding from each Fund that has approved your project - up to your cap. On top of that, top projects win cash prizes from the Endowment.

**What happens if nobody buys ART or Artifacts?**

Then creators don’t raise much. Simple as that. Markets don’t reward ideas no one supports. But the model scales with demand: more buyers  bigger Endowment  bigger prizes  more creators.

**Why would brands sponsor a Fund instead of just running their own grant program?**

Because Artizen is cheaper and more efficient. Brands don’t need to manage applications, reviews, or payouts. The community curates projects, and match funding multiplies their money. They get more impact for less overhead.

**What stops big creators from dominating every season?**

By “big creators,” we mean those with large fanbases or access to capital. Artizen has several safeguards to keep them from swamping the system:

- **Time-Invested voting.** Everyone gets a voice, but not all voices are equally loud. New users start with limited voting power. Fans, sponsors, and proven creators who contribute more earn more power - but each additional vote costs more. And every vote must be cast manually, forcing people to invest their time as well as their influence.
- **Fund Director oversight.** Even if a creator has a massive audience, their project must still meet the Fund’s eligibility rules. Fund Directors can remove submissions that don’t fit the spirit or requirements of their Fund.
- **Match funding caps.** Each approved project can unlock up to 30% of a Fund’s balance. Caps shrink as money is claimed, so no one can drain the whole pool - even with big wallets. That means a wealthy creator can’t simply buy up their own Artifacts (or have rich friends do it) to drain the whole pool. Once their cap is reached, they can’t unlock more.
- **Prize money distribution.** Endowment prizes are spread across projects using a formula that rewards top performers while ensuring everyone ranked still gets something. This softens the edge of big creators winning everything.

**How does Artizen prevent fake voting and manipulation?**

It’s a real challenge, but we’ve built in safeguards:

- **Phone verification.** To vote, you must verify a phone number. That makes mass fake accounts harder to spin up.
- **Limited free votes.** New users get only a small batch of free votes, which run out quickly. To gain more, you need to contribute money or succeed as a creator.
- **Quadratic voting.** Our flavor of quadratic voting makes each extra vote more expensive. Even “whales” face diminishing returns. And every vote has to be clicked manually - forcing people to spend time, not just money.

Together, these checks make it costly and time-consuming to cheat, while rewarding real engagement and community support.

**What’s to stop Artizen from changing the rules whenever it wants?**

Some rules can’t change at all. The ART token runs on immutable smart contracts - issuance, floor price, and seller fees are hardwired. No one, including us, can rewrite those rules.

Other policies - like how Funds operate, how votes are counted, or how prizes are distributed - can evolve. But we don’t flip switches overnight. Changes are usually discussed for a long time and shaped by community feedback. The goal is to refine the system, not surprise people.

Long term, our aim is to move more and more of Artizen onto smart contracts - pieces of code that are autonomous and unchangeable. But we’ll only do that once we’re very confident a mechanism is ready to be frozen forever. This is the standard progressive decentralization approach: start flexible while we learn, then harden rules in code once they prove themselves.

So yes, some policies can shift, but always with the mission in mind - and always moving toward greater immutability over time.

**Why should anyone believe this will last?**

Because it’s built like Bitcoin - transparent rules, long-term incentives, and an engine that’s designed to sustain itself. Every season grows the Endowment, every sale adds Artifacts to the treasury, and every prize awarded to creators fuels demand. The system compounds over time.

The sprint extends automatically. This "fair finish" rule ensures the displaced leader has a chance to respond. The sprint only ends when no more lead changes happen during the extension window.
