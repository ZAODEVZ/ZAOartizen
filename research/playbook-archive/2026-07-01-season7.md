##  Quickstart

Artizen is a funding platform for breakthroughs in art, science, technology, and culture.

**Each season:**

- People launch new Funds to support their community.
- Mission-aligned brands and individuals sponsor Funds.
- Creators submit projects to any Funds they qualify for.
- Fund admins curate the projects they want to back. Every approved project also gets baseline match from the Artizen Endowment, so no project is left without fuel.
- Projects sell Artifacts, a new kind of digital collectible, to their fans.
- Every $1 of sales (and every $1 of sponsorship) unlocks match funding from curated Funds and the Endowment, scaled by that week’s Match Multiple set by Artizen.
- The community casts Boosts on the projects and Funds they want to see win. Boosts are powered by Boost Points, earned by donating to the Endowment or holding ART.

**Each week is a Fund Drive (Thursday to Thursday):**

- Two leaderboards run in parallel: projects vs projects, Funds vs Funds.
- Your rank is your Boost Score = (sales + match unlocked) × boost points received. Multiplicative, so strong sales and community heat are both required.
- Top ranks win weekly cash prizes from the Artizen Endowment.
- Season-long totals roll up to a big end-of-season prize when the season closes.

##  Gameplay

Whether you're a creator, community leader, or sponsor, here's how to play the game of Artizen.

###  Setup

#### ENDOWMENT

The Artizen Endowment is the engine behind every cash prize and most of the match funding Artizen puts on the table (sponsors who back Community Funds add the rest). When you donate to it, you earn ART tokens and Boost Points in return: every $1 donated earns 100 Boost Points ($10 = 1,000, $100 = 10,000). ART gives you a weekly recharge of Boost Points, and Boost Points are how you push the projects and Funds you love up the leaderboard. Fund the engine, get the fuel to play.

#### FUNDS

Funds back the projects that matter to a community. Two ways to play:

- **Launch a Fund** to support creators around a cause, place, or theme you care about.
- **Sponsor a Fund** to grow its match pool, reach its audience, earn rewards, and help it climb the leaderboard toward cash prizes.

#### PROJECTS

Projects are the breakthroughs being funded. Two ways to play:

- **Submit a project** to any Funds you qualify for, then sell cultural Artifacts to your fans.
- **Buy Artifacts** from projects you love. Every purchase supports the creator, unlocks match funding on top, and helps the project climb the leaderboard toward cash prizes.

###  Curation

**Get curated**
Fund admins review submissions and curate the projects that fit. Get curated by as many Community Funds as you can; each one adds to your available match funding. Every project is also auto-added to the Artizen Endowment, so you always have a baseline match to play with.

**Earn Boost Points**
Donate to the Endowment or hold ART to earn Boost Points. Every $1 you donate to the Endowment earns you 100 Boost Points (so $10 earns 1,000 and $100 earns 10,000).

**Cast Boosts**
Spend Boost Points to Boost the projects and Funds you want to see win cash prizes. Boosts raise their Boost Score, and pushes them up the leaderboard.

###  Competition

Every week we have a new Fund Drive (Thursday to Thursday) with two leaderboards running in parallel:

- Projects compete against other projects for the top project cash prizes.
- Community Funds compete against other Funds for the top Fund cash prizes.

Both leaderboards work the same way:

**Drive activity**
Projects sell Artifacts; Funds attract sponsorships. Every $1 raised unlocks match funding, scaled by that week's Match Multiple set by Artizen (e.g. 2× means each $1 unlocks $2 in match), up to your available cap.

**Rally Boosts**
Ask your community to Boost your project or Boost your Fund.

**Climb the leaderboard**
Your spot is set by your Boost Score, calculated as: (sales + match) × boost points received ÷ 100. Dollars always count and boosts always count, so stacking both is how you climb fastest.

**Win weekly cash prizes**
Every ranked project and Fund wins some cash, but most of the pool goes to the top. The #1 rank takes the biggest prize, #2 gets half of #1, and #3 gets half of #2. From there the prizes flatten out, so everyone further down the leaderboard still earns a steadier, more even share. Then the board resets and a new drive begins.

###  Example

####  The Bloom Fund Drive

Theme: creativity that makes a community feel alive.

It’s a fresh weekly Fund Drive, and two races run side by side: Funds compete against Funds, and projects compete against projects. Artizen sets this week’s Match Multiple to 3×, which means every $1 anyone backs unlocks $3 of match funding on top.

####  The Fund race

Funds are run by community leaders who curate a slate of projects. They compete for sponsor dollars: the more a Fund raises, the bigger the match pool it can hand to its projects. Two Funds are going head to head this week.

 **Public Art Fund (Josh)** rallies local businesses for $4K in sponsorships  +$12K match at 3×  $16K driven. His community Boosts the Fund hard: 8,000 boost points.
Boost Score = $16K × 8,000 ÷ 100 = 1.28M

 **Maker Fund (Priya)** raises even more, $6K in sponsorships  +$18K match  $24K driven, but barely rallies Boosts: 300 boost points.
Boost Score = $24K × 300 ÷ 100 = 720K

Maker Fund raised more raw dollars, but Public Art Fund wins the Fund race on community heat. Boosts, not just dollars, decide rank.

####  The Project race

Now zoom into the Public Art Fund. It curates three projects, and every sponsor dollar Josh raised widened the match those projects can unlock, in real time. Each project sells Artifacts (matched 3×) and rallies fans to Boost.

 **Tree Sculpture (Sarah)** sells $1K  +$3K match  $4K raised · 10,000 boosts
Boost Score = $4K × 10,000 ÷ 100 = 400K

 **Little Library (Zach)** sells just $200  +$600 match  $800 raised, but his fans flood in: 9,000 boosts
Boost Score = $800 × 9,000 ÷ 100 = 72K

 **Street Mural (Victor)** sells the most, $1.5K  +$4.5K match  $6K raised, but barely rallies Boosts: 100 boosts
Boost Score = $6K × 100 ÷ 100 = 6K

Victor sold the most dollars yet finished last, because Boosts multiply your dollars. Sarah’s sales and heat vaulted her to #1, and Zach’s boost-rich crowd beat Victor’s bigger sales.

####  How the cash prizes pay out

Both races award cash. Every ranked project and Fund wins something, but the top of each board earns the most: #2 gets half of #1, #3 gets half of #2, then it flattens into a steadier share for everyone below.

In this drive’s project race the prizes land like this:

-  Tree Sculpture wins $2,000
-  Little Library wins $1,000 (half of #1)
-  Street Mural wins $500 (half of #2)

Ranks 4 and below still earn cash, just on a flatter curve (think $250, $220, $200, and so on). Victor sold the most all week and still walks away with a prize; he just left the bigger ones on the table by skipping Boosts.

The Fund race pays out the same way, on its own (larger) pool:

-  Public Art Fund wins $4,000
-  Maker Fund wins $2,000 (half of #1)
-  third-place Fund wins $1,000 (half of #2), and so on down the board

Here’s the compounding twist: a Fund’s cash prize doesn’t get paid out as cash, it rolls into that Fund’s match pool for next week’s drive. Josh’s $4,000 win means the Public Art Fund starts next week with $4,000 more match to hand its projects, before he raises a single new sponsor dollar. Win a strong week, and your projects get an even bigger head start the next.

####  What carries into next week

- Sponsor and sales dollars unlock match instantly for curated projects mid-drive.
- A Fund’s cash prize rolls into its match pool next week (see above), so a strong week compounds into a stronger one.
- Unused match never vanishes: project leftovers return to the Fund, Fund leftovers roll forward, and the rest returns to the Endowment.

 **The takeaway:** Boost Score multiplies dollars by community heat. Sales and sponsorships fuel your match; Boosts fuel your rank; you need both to win big. Get curated by as many Funds as you can, sell Artifacts, and rally your people to Boost you. Then do it again, because every Fund Drive is a fresh leaderboard, and this week’s wins feed next week’s match.

##  Projects

We fund bold ideas in art, science, technology, and culture, at any stage, in any medium. From oil paintings to open-source code, from raw sparks to finished works.

What we look for:

- **Originality.** Is it fresh? Surprising? Unlike anything we’ve seen?
- **Impact.** Will it change lives, revitalize communities, or regenerate ecosystems?
- **Feasibility.** Can you pull it off with the team, time, and money you’ve got?

Anyone can submit, for free. But if you want to unlock match funding and win cash prizes, you’ll need to rise above the rest. Here’s how.

###  Create a Profile

To get started, head to the Artizen home page and create your account. Once you’re signed in, you can create a project profile from your user profile page, or by clicking Submit on the home page. Your project profile is the public face of your project, the first thing seen by your fans, friends, and supporters. So make it dope.

**REQUIRED**

- **Project Title:** Short and specific (max 75 characters)
- **Logline:** One-sentence hook that captures your big idea (max 140 characters)
- **Tags:** Describe your project's focus and field
- **What are you making?** Say it plainly, like you’re talking to a friend (250 characters)
- **How will it impact the world?** Tell us why it matters (250 characters)
- **What progress have you made?** Show momentum (250 characters)
- **Why are you the right person?** Why are you uniquely suited? (250 characters)
- **Artifact:** An image, gif, or video that captures the soul of your project. See guidelines

**OPTIONAL (but powerful)**

- **Featured Video:** Link to a YouTube or Vimeo video.
- **Rewards:** Power-ups and perks for fans who buy your Artifacts. See guidelines
- **Pitch Deck:** Upload a PDF that goes deeper into your story. See guidelines

###  Get Funded

Once your profile’s ready, it's time to start fundraising.

#### SUBMIT TO FUNDS

Browse the active Funds and apply to every one you qualify for. From the home page you can find them three ways: tap the search icon and enter tags or keywords, scroll down to the Funds leaderboard to see them all, or click Submit to pick your project and pull up the full list of open Funds (with search built in). Read each description, study the eligibility criteria, then tailor your submission and explain why you’re a good fit. The more Funds you submit to, the more shots you have at getting approved.

#### GET APPROVED

Fund admins review every submission and decide whether your project fits their eligibility criteria. Community support plays a big role here. Boosts and engagement signal how deep and broad your following is and help your project get full consideration. But they don’t auto-approve you. Final decisions always rest with Fund Directors and admins, who approve or remove projects based on fit with their mission.

#### RAISE MONEY

Once you’re approved, you’re in the game. Start selling Artifacts to your fans. Every $1 you raise unlocks match funding from each Fund backing your project, at that week’s Match Multiple, while the Fund’s match lasts. The more you sell, the more match you unlock, and the higher your Boost Score climbs. Top-ranked projects also win weekly cash prizes.

###  Fund Drives

Every week, Artizen runs a Fund Drive: a fresh, time-limited competition to unlock match funding and win cash prizes. Each drive starts a new leaderboard, so every week is a clean shot at winning, no matter how last week went.

####  Your available match funding (your goal for the week)

At the start of every Fund Drive, your project is given a pool of available match funding. This is the number to beat. It comes from two places: the Artizen Endowment (set by that week’s Match Multiple) and every Fund that has curated you.

Each Fund you’re curated for contributes a slice of its own available match to you. The percentage you’re allocated from a curated Fund is based on your sales over the last 4 weeks compared to the other projects in that Fund: more recent sales than the other projects means a larger match allocation. Each Fund also has a floor and ceiling percentage, which sets the minimum and maximum a single project can receive that week. The floor and ceiling are based on the number of curated projects in the Fund. All allocations are calculated so that no more than 100% of a Fund is handed out in any given week. So getting curated by more Funds, and staying active with recent sales, both deepen the pool of match you can unlock.

That whole pool is reserved for you for the entire drive. Nobody else can take it. The number you see on your project’s Fund Drive card is exactly what your community needs to unlock before the clock runs out.

####  How you unlock it

Match unlocks as your fans buy Artifacts, at that week’s Match Multiple. At a 3× multiple, every $1 a fan spends unlocks $3 of your available match, for $4 total raised on that dollar, until your pool runs out.

Whatever available match you don’t unlock by the end of the drive is returned to the Endowment and the curated Funds. It isn’t lost forever: it flows back into the pots that fund next week. Your next drive then opens with a freshly calculated amount of available match, which may be more or less than this week’s, depending on how much money is left and how well you performed.

####  Boosts, Boost Score, and rank

Your community also casts Boost Points for your project to push you up the leaderboard. (You and your supporters earn Boost Points in lots of ways: holding ART, donating to the Artizen Endowment, completing your user profile, attending Artizen events, and other prosocial activities that add value to the community.)

Your rank is set by your Boost Score: (sales + match unlocked) × boost points received ÷ 100. It balances money raised against community heat, so you need both to climb. Dollars alone or boosts alone will only get you so far.

####  Cash prizes

When the drive ends, cash prizes are awarded by rank. Every ranked project wins something, but most of the pool goes to the top: the #1 project takes the biggest prize, #2 earns half of #1, and #3 earns half of #2, then the rest flatten into a steadier share. Your prize is added to your project’s total money raised at the end of the drive. For a full worked example, see the Gameplay section.

####  Your Fund Drive card

The Fund Drive card on your project page is scoped to your project only. When someone boosts or buys on that card, they’re contributing to you. Want to see the math behind it? Tap the Fund Drive title on the card and a popup breaks down the specifics: your available match, what’s been unlocked, your Boost Score, and your current rank.

####  Timing and the fair finish

Drives end Thursdays at 11:00 AM PST. The next Fund Drive begins as close to immediately after as possible, so the rhythm is weekly and continuous.

**Fair finish.** A drive doesn’t end the instant the clock hits zero. It only closes once the #1 project has held first place for a full 5 minutes. If someone overtakes the leader in the final moments, the clock effectively keeps going until the top spot stabilizes, so the win is earned, not sniped at the buzzer.

####  Strategy

- **Boost early and often.** Boost Points compound your Boost Score all week, so the sooner the heat starts, the higher you climb.
- **Raise all week.** Every sale unlocks match and lifts your score. Steady selling beats a single last-minute push.
- **Have your community ready for the finish.** Because of the 5-minute fair finish, the very end is where ranks are won and lost. Line up your supporters so they can boost and buy on cue when it matters most.
- **Get curated by more Funds.** More Funds backing you means a deeper pool of available match to unlock in a single drive.

###  Payouts

How and when you get paid.

Each Artifact costs $10 (plus applicable fees at checkout). Artifact sales count toward your season payout. After the season ends, we calculate final totals (sales + match awards + prizes), complete reviews, and then issue payouts.

Payouts are issued after the season ends so we can finalize results, confirm cleared transactions, and process payouts accurately. It also keeps the game fair. If money went out mid-season, a bad actor could spin up a project, buy their own Artifacts to unlock match funding and win cash prizes, collect the payout, then round-trip that same money back into the next Fund Drive. Settling once at the end of the season closes that loop.

How much a $1 sale unlocks depends on the week’s Match Multiple, while money lasts. At a 3× multiple, a $1 sale unlocks $3 in match, for $4 total raised. That match is pulled from all the Funds you’re curated for, in proportion to each Fund’s size, so getting curated by more Funds simply gives you a deeper pool of available match. Match awards are tracked throughout the season and included in your final payout. Prize money is added to your project’s total money raised at the end of each weekly Fund Drive, based on your final rank that week. The only exception is the season’s final week: instead of starting fresh, every project is ranked on season totals, using a Boost Score built from total sales, total match, and total boost points received.

At the end of the season, you’ll receive one combined payout:

- Artifact Sales +
- Match Funding +
- Any Cash Prizes

After a season ends, payouts are processed in batches following a review and audit period. Most creators are paid within a few weeks, though some payouts may take longer depending on size, review requirements, and payment clearing timelines. Artizen collects all fees upfront when Artifacts are purchased or Funds are sponsored, so there are no additional deductions at payout beyond any actual transfer costs.

Artifact sales, match awards, and your weekly Fund Drive prizes are all paid in US dollars to a bank account, or in USDC to your Artizen wallet. Weekly prize money is simply added to your project’s total money raised. The one exception is the season’s final overall prize, awarded in the last week: that is paid in ART, the Artizen token, giving creators an ownership stake in the platform.

###  Rewards for Fans

Once published, you have the option to add Rewards for fans who back your project.

By default, fans get:

- **Artifacts:** 1 Artifact for every $10 they spend
- **Impact:** Every $1 unlocks match funding and cash prizes

But the magic happens when you offer rewards of your own. Just like Kickstarter, you can offer perks based on how much fans give. Digital downloads. Merch. Zoom hangs. VIP access. Behind-the-scenes drops. Whatever makes sense for your project and audience.

**BEST PRACTICES**

- **Keep it real:** Only offer what you can deliver.
- **Make it special:** Give fans something they can’t get anywhere else.
- **Price smart:** Balance reward value with what you need to raise.
- **Use tiers:** Offer multiple reward tiers to match different budgets.
- **Great image:** Keep it simple and clean. It should look great even at small sizes.

While you can add as many rewards as you want, we recommend 3 to 5. Enough to give people options without overwhelming them.

###  Fund Drives

Funding on Artizen happens through Fund Drives: weekly, time-limited competitions. Each drive is your chance to rally sponsors and supporters around your Fund, unlock match funding from the Endowment, and compete to win cash prizes for your Fund. Everything you raise and win gets pooled and made available to the creators and projects you curate. Here’s how to run a winning drive.

####  Unlock match funding for your Fund

At the start of every drive, Artizen calculates how much available match funding your Fund has access to. The more sponsor money your Fund has been bringing in, the more match the Endowment makes available to you. Specifically, we look at your Fund’s trailing 4-week average of sponsor contributions relative to every other Fund: the more active your Fund has been at getting sponsors on board, the larger your share of available match funding. So your first job each drive is simple: get sponsors on board. Sponsor dollars aren’t paid out directly; they are pooled and used to unlock match from the Endowment, deepening the support available to your curated projects. The Endowment sets a Match Multiple each drive, and that multiple determines how much match a $1 sponsorship unlocks (for example, at 3× a $1 sale unlocks $3 in match) until your available match runs dry.

Treat that available match as your goal for the week: unlock all of it. Bring in enough sponsors that, at the current Match Multiple, every dollar of available match from the Endowment gets unlocked. That is how you grow your Fund and create the biggest impact for the creators you care about.

####  Win cash prizes for your Fund

Funds compete against other Funds on a single global Funds leaderboard. Your Fund climbs it by combining two things during the drive: sponsorship (the money you raise and the match you unlock) and boosts (the support your community rallies behind your Fund and its projects). The higher your Fund ranks, the bigger the cash prize it wins. We use a halving-style curve, so the top Funds earn the most, but every ranked Fund gets paid. A Fund’s prize doesn’t pay out as cash to you; it rolls into your Fund’s match pool for the next drive, deepening the support you can offer creators the following week. Win, and you have more to give.

####  Timing and the fair finish

Drives end Thursdays at 11:00 AM PST. The next Fund Drive begins as close to immediately after as possible, so the rhythm is weekly and continuous.

**Fair finish.** A drive only closes once the #1 spot has held for a full 5 minutes, so a leader can’t be sniped at the buzzer. Rally your community to be ready for the finish.

####  How to run a strong drive

- **Get sponsors on board early.** Sponsorship is what unlocks your match pool and lifts your Fund up the leaderboard. The earlier in the drive, the better.
- **Curate great projects.** Approve strong projects before and at the start of the drive so they have the full week to unlock the match you’ve built.
- **Rally everyone who cares.** Fund Director, curators, fans, and supporters all boosting and backing your projects is what climbs the leaderboard and wins the prize.

For the full worked example of how match, boosts, and prizes fit together, see the Gameplay section.

##  Artifacts

A new kind of digital collectible.

Artifacts capture the essence of a project and its intended impact on the world. They can be a sketch, concept art, looping gif, photo, animation, video - any media that marks a milestone and channels the vibe of a project.

To be clear, Artifacts are not projects themselves. Rather, they are unique works of art that represent the "big ideas" behind a project.

###  Collecting Artifacts

Artifacts cost $10 and are sold as open edition NFTs throughout the season. You can buy as many as you want from any project, and your purchase supports the project and increases what the creator can earn this season. Each sale also unlocks additional match funding, boosts, and prizes - amplifying your impact.

For every Artifact purchased, we mint multiple copies:

- One for the Fan who bought it
- One for the Creator
- One for Artizen

This way, everyone has skin in the game - and a share in the upside. At the end of the season, sales close, and the total number of Artifacts minted becomes the fixed supply.

###  Investing in Impact

Artifacts aren’t equity. They don’t give you shares, control, or IP rights in a project. With Artifacts, you don’t own the project - you own the impact.

Like all works of art, the value of an Artifact comes from its aesthetics, rarity, and emotional resonance. But its true worth depends on the impact of the project it represents. The greater the impact, the more valuable the Artifact.

Most Artifacts will go to zero - because most projects fail. But a few will transform the world. And those will be priceless.

###  How ART Works

The Endowment runs on a Revnet, an autonomous financial engine built on Juicebox. Think of it less like a slot machine and more like a vending machine you can trust: put money in, and you know exactly what comes out. The rules are immutable, on-chain, and fully autonomous. No secret levers.

- **Dollars in, ART out.** The Revnet mints new ART or buys it on the market, whichever is cheaper. Your dollars stay locked in the Revnet.

- **Revenue fuels buybacks.** Platform revenue and Treasury funds are used to buy back ART, adding steady demand independent of speculation.
- **Price doubles on a schedule.** The mint price doubles on a fixed timeline, forever, rewarding early supporters and creating scarcity over time.
- **Cash-out guarantee.** Burn ART to redeem US dollars from the Revnet, or sell on the open market.

##  FAQ

Learning the rules to any new game can be confusing. We get it. Artizen mixes elements of crowdfunding, crypto, and community competition, so of course there are a lot of moving parts. No question is dumb. If you’re unsure about something, you’re not alone. This FAQ clears up the most common points of confusion so you can focus on playing and winning the game.

###  Artifacts

#### What exactly is an Artifact?

An Artifact is a digital collectible that represents a project. Each sale supports the creator, unlocks match funding and prizes, and becomes part of a fixed edition at season’s end.

#### If I buy an Artifact, do I own the project?

No. Artifacts don’t give you control, equity, or rights. You own the collectible itself. Its value comes from the project’s cultural or social impact, not financial claims.

#### Why would I buy more than one Artifact?

More Artifacts mean more rewards and more direct support for the creator. Plus, if the project succeeds, your Artifacts could appreciate in value and become culturally important collectibles. Think of Andy Warhol’s silkscreen prints. At the time they were just multiples, but as his work reshaped the art world, those prints skyrocketed in value. If a project on Artizen changes the world of art, science, technology, or culture, you’ll wish you held more than one.

#### Why should fans spend $10 on an Artifact that might be worthless?

Most Artifacts will go to zero. That’s honest. But a few projects will change the world. Those Artifacts will be priceless. Fans buy both for the upside and to support projects they believe in. The $10 isn’t charity, it’s skin in the game.

#### Are Artifacts regulated as securities?

No. Artifacts are unique works of art, open-edition NFTs under the hood. Art is the last great unregulated market. Anyone can buy and sell art. That’s why Artifacts are digital collectibles, not equity, share, stocks, or securities.

###  Projects

#### How does match funding work?

Every Fund Drive, Artizen sets a Match Multiple for each Fund. When you sell an Artifact, your sales are matched at that multiple from the Funds that have approved your project, up to the match available that week. Get approved by more Funds, and sell while match is still available, to unlock more.

#### How is prize money different from match funding?

Match funding is tied directly to sales: you sell, Artizen matches it at the Fund Drive’s Match Multiple. Prize money is different. It is cash from the Endowment, awarded each Fund Drive based on your Boost Score, which combines how much you raised (sales plus match) with how many boosts your project received. Every Fund Drive there are two leaderboards: one ranking projects against projects, and one ranking Funds against Funds. The top performers on each win the biggest prizes.

#### How and when do creators actually get paid?

Payouts go out after each season ends, so all sales, match, and prizes can be audited and paid together. It normally takes a few weeks for payouts to start, and up to 60 days for everything to be audited and cleared.

You choose how you get paid: US dollars to a bank account, or USDC (a U.S. dollar stablecoin) to a crypto wallet. Artifact sales, match funding, and weekly Fund Drive cash prizes are all paid this way. The one exception is the season finale prize, which is paid in ART, Artizen’s token.

#### Can creators buy Artifacts from their own project?

Yes. Creators are allowed to support their own projects. This is common when creators use personal funds or grants that can’t be routed directly through Artizen.

To keep the game fair, all sales, match funding, and prizes are held until the season ends, so creators cannot recycle funds or artificially amplify results mid-season.

#### Who decides which projects get curated?

Each Fund is run by its Fund Director and a team of curators. They review every submission and approve the projects that meet their eligibility criteria and fit the spirit of their Fund.

Boosts matter: they signal how much real support a project has and help curators prioritize what to review, but boosts do not auto-approve anyone. Final decisions always rest with the Fund’s Director and curators.

#### What if a creator raises money but doesn’t deliver?

That risk is real. Creators keep their sales, but must use awards to advance their project. We expect updates, and projects can be removed if they misrepresent themselves. Failure is part of discovery. Markets reward what works and punish what doesn’t. That’s how progress happens.

#### What if a creator takes the money and disappears?

It will happen. Risk is real. Our rules require creators to use awards to advance their projects, but rules alone can’t stop bad actors. Scammers gonna scam. The best guard against this is reputation. Creators who don’t deliver will be booted from the platform, and their chances of raising again will vanish. Over time, the market weeds out bad faith players and rewards those who consistently build and deliver.

###  Payouts

#### Why do payouts happen after the season ends?

Payouts happen after the season ends to keep the game fair. If creators could receive funds during a season, they could recycle that money back into their own project to unlock more match funding or climb the leaderboard, creating an unfair feedback loop. Holding funds until the season ends prevents this kind of self-reinforcing behavior and ensures that all match funding and prizes are earned through real, external support.

Delaying payouts also allows us to finalize rankings, confirm Artifact sales and match funding across Funds, and ensure that all payments have fully cleared before money is distributed. Together, these safeguards protect creators, sponsors, and the integrity of the competition and makes traditional wash sales and self-reinforcing loops impossible during a season.

#### How long will it take to receive my payout?

Payouts begin shortly after each season ends and are processed in batches. Most creators receive their payout within a few weeks. In some cases, especially for larger payouts or where additional review is required, the process can take longer, but all payouts are completed within 60 days of the season ending.

Before releasing funds, we finalize season results, confirm Artifact sales and match funding, ensure leaderboard rankings and prize allocations are correct, and verify that all credit card, bank transfers, and other payments have fully cleared. We also complete required tax and compliance checks so payouts are issued correctly and lawfully.

Our goal is to pay creators as quickly as possible while maintaining a fair and trusted system for everyone. If we need anything from you to complete your payout, we’ll reach out directly. Otherwise, you’ll receive a confirmation as soon as your payout is on the way.

#### Why do some creators get paid earlier than others?

Payouts are processed in batches, and different payouts require different levels of review. Smaller payouts are usually faster to verify and can often be processed sooner. Larger payouts may require additional review, compliance checks, or clearing time, and in some cases may be handled separately. The goal is to pay everyone as quickly as possible while keeping the process accurate and compliant.

#### What do I need to provide in order to get paid?

Before we can release a payout, we need you to confirm who is legally receiving the funds and provide the appropriate tax documentation. We may also ask you to confirm basic compliance acknowledgments required to process payments safely and lawfully. If anything is missing or doesn’t match, we’ll follow up, and your payout may be delayed until it’s resolved.

#### Are payouts taxable?

Yes. In most cases, payouts are considered taxable income. Artizen does not provide tax advice, and you are responsible for determining how to report these funds based on your local laws and circumstances. We may be required to collect tax forms and issue tax reporting documents depending on your jurisdiction and the type of recipient.

#### How are payouts issued, and what’s the difference between USDC and ART?

Artifact sales, match funding, and weekly Fund Drive cash prizes are all paid in US dollars, either to a bank account or as USDC (a U.S. dollar stablecoin) to a crypto wallet. The creator decides which. The only payout in ART is the season finale prize. ART is Artizen’s native token and represents a stake in the Artizen Endowment and platform. Some winners hold their ART long-term if they believe Artizen will keep growing; others redeem or sell some for liquidity. What you do with ART is up to you.

#### Why is the season finale prize paid in ART?

To be clear on what’s paid how: all weekly Fund Drive money, sales, match, and cash prizes, is US dollar denominated. Only the season finale prize is paid in ART. We pay the finale in ART because it aligns the season’s top creators with the long-term growth of the Endowment. Instead of a one-time cash payout, ART gives them a stake in the system that funds future seasons, with the option to participate in the upside as Artizen grows.

The Fund Director owns the Fund. They launch it, set its eligibility rules, and recruit a team of curators to help run it, so most Funds have several curators working alongside the Director. #### How are Fund prizes calculated?

The top three ranks are weighted heavily. On both the project and the Fund leaderboards, each of the top three prizes is double the size of the rank below it: first place is double second, second is double third. From rank four down to the last ranked spot, prizes step down more gently, on a linear curve, so every project that ranks still earns a meaningful cut. The goal is to reward the very best while making sure no one who ranks walks away empty-handed.

Unused match doesn’t disappear. Within a Fund Drive, match a project doesn’t use flows to the rest of that Fund’s projects; match a Fund doesn’t use rolls into that Fund’s next weekly drive; and anything still left at season’s end returns to the Endowment to seed future Funds.

###  Platform

#### How does Artizen make money?

This mix of sales fees and ART revenue funds the platform. More importantly, it aligns our success with yours: the healthier the Endowment and the more creators thrive, the stronger Artizen becomes.

#### Isn’t this just Kickstarter with extra steps?

No. Kickstarter is a one-off tool to collect money from your own network. Unless you go viral, that’s the limit. Artizen takes that same effort and amplifies it. Every dollar you raise from a fan is matched by the Funds that approved you, at a Match Multiple Artizen sets each week. On top of that, the top projects each Fund Drive win cash prizes from the Endowment.

Then creators don’t raise much. Simple as that. Markets don’t reward ideas no one supports. But the model scales with demand: more buyers  bigger Endowment  bigger prizes  more creators.

#### Why would brands sponsor a Fund instead of just running their own grant program?

Because Artizen is cheaper and more efficient. Brands don’t need to manage applications, reviews, or payouts. The community curates projects, and match funding multiplies their money. They get more impact for less overhead.

#### What stops big creators from dominating every season?

By “big creators,” we mean those with large fanbases or access to capital. Artizen has several safeguards to keep them from swamping the system:

- **Boost Points cost more the more you use.** Everyone gets a voice, but not all voices are equally loud. Boosts are spent from a balance, and each additional boost on the same project costs more, so raw spending power hits diminishing returns. Boosts must be cast manually too, forcing people to invest their time as well as their influence.
- **Fund Director oversight.** Even if a creator has a massive audience, their project must still meet the Fund’s eligibility rules. Fund Directors can remove submissions that don’t fit the spirit or requirements of their Fund.
- **Match is capped each week.** Match for a Fund Drive is limited and shared across that Fund’s projects, so no single project can drain the whole pool, even with a big wallet. A wealthy creator can’t simply buy up their own Artifacts (or have rich friends do it) to swallow the match. Once the week’s match is used, there is no more to unlock.
- **Boost Score, not raw spend.** Prizes are awarded on Boost Score, which multiplies what you raised by the boosts you earned. A creator can’t win on money alone; they need genuine community support behind them too.

#### How does Artizen prevent fake boosting and manipulation?

It’s a real challenge, but we’ve built in safeguards:

- **Phone verification.** To boost, you must verify a phone number. That makes mass fake accounts harder to spin up.
- **Limited free boosts.** New users get only a small batch of free boosts, which run out quickly. To gain more, you need to contribute money or succeed as a creator.
- **Rising boost cost.** Each extra boost on a project costs more, so even “whales” face diminishing returns. And every boost has to be clicked manually, forcing people to spend time, not just money.

Together, these checks make it costly and time-consuming to cheat, while rewarding real engagement and community support.

#### What’s to stop Artizen from changing the rules whenever it wants?

Some rules can’t change at all. The ART token runs on immutable smart contracts: issuance, floor price, and seller fees are hardwired. No one, including us, can rewrite those rules.

Other policies, like how Funds operate, how boosts are counted, or how prizes are distributed, can evolve. But we don’t flip switches overnight. Changes are usually discussed for a long time and shaped by community feedback. The goal is to refine the system, not surprise people.

Long term, our aim is to move more and more of Artizen onto smart contracts, pieces of code that are autonomous and unchangeable. But we’ll only do that once we’re very confident a mechanism is ready to be frozen forever. This is the standard progressive decentralization approach: start flexible while we learn, then harden rules in code once they prove themselves.

So yes, some policies can shift, but always with the mission in mind, and always moving toward greater immutability over time.

#### Why should anyone believe this will last?

Because it’s built like Bitcoin: transparent rules, long-term incentives, and an engine that’s designed to sustain itself. Every season grows the Endowment, every sale adds Artifacts to the treasury, and every prize awarded to creators fuels demand. The system compounds over time.

#### What is a Fund Drive?

A Fund Drive is Artizen’s weekly competition. Each one runs Friday through Thursday. Projects compete against other projects, and Funds compete against other Funds, on two leaderboards. At the close, the top performers win cash prizes from the Endowment based on their Boost Score. Then a new drive begins, with fresh Boost Points and a fresh shot at the top.

###  Boosts

#### What are Boosts?

Boosts are how the community shows support. You spend Boost Points on the projects you believe in, and a project’s boosts feed its Boost Score, which helps decide how much it wins each Fund Drive. The more genuine support a project earns, the higher it can climb.

#### What are Boost Points and Boost Score?

Boost Points are what you spend to boost projects. Everyone gets some for free, holding ART tops you up each week, and the more you contribute the more you get. Boost Score is how a project’s prize is decided each Fund Drive: it combines how much the project raised (Artifact sales plus match) with how many boosts it received. You need both real money and real support to win big.
